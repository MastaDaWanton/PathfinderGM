> I kiss her

She meets you halfway. The first kiss is careful, almost a question, her mouth soft and
a little dry; the second is not careful at all. Her hand finds the back of your neck and
holds you there, fingers threading into your hair, and you feel her breath catch against
your lips when you draw her in by the waist.

You had thought about this. You had not thought about how warm she would be, or how she
would laugh, low and surprised, when your nose bumped hers, and then kiss you harder as
if to make up for it. Under your palms her back is all long muscle, and it shifts as she
leans into you, pressing you back a step until something solid stops you.

She pulls away just far enough to look at you. Her eyes are dark, the pupils wide, and
there is colour high on her cheeks. Neither of you says anything. Then her fingers are
at the laces of your shirt, working them loose one cross at a time, slowly, watching
your face the whole while as though the knots are an excuse to take her time.

Cool air finds your skin where the cloth parts, and then her hands do, flat and warm
over your ribs, over the beat of your heart, which she must be able to feel hammering.
She smiles at that. You kiss the smile, and the edge of her jaw, and the place below her
ear that makes her go still and then shiver all at once, and her hands tighten on you.

> I pull him closer

and warm against yours, his breathing ragged, and when his mouth finds yours again there
is nothing patient in it. Your hands are under his shirt before you have decided to put

Clothes become an obstacle you both resent. He gets his shirt over his head and throws

lock of hair fallen across his eyes. You push it back. He turns his face into your palm
and kisses it, and the tenderness of that, in the middle of all this hunger, undoes you

What follows is heat and rhythm and the creak of the frame beneath you, his voice low
and rough at your ear, your name and then no words at all. Time loosens. There is only
the press of him, the way his breath stutters when you move against him, the slow,
climbing ache of it, and his hand finding yours on the blanket and holding on hard.

> I fumble with the buckles of their armour

The buckles win the first round. They are stiff with old oil and set where no one could
reach them alone, and after the third one defeats you they start to laugh, a helpless
sound that shakes the whole cuirass.

"Here," they say, and turn their back to you, lifting their arms. "Left side first. It
sticks."

It does stick. You get it loose with a tug that nearly knocks them over, and the
breastplate comes away in your hands, heavier than you expected; you set it down with
exaggerated care, and they laugh again, and then stop laughing when you step close
behind them and put your mouth to the bare skin at the nape of their neck.

The padded jack beneath is damp with sweat and smells of leather and the road. You work
it off their shoulders. Underneath they are marked the way fighters are: a pale seam
along one shoulder blade, a bruise going yellow at the hip, a strap-mark worn into the
skin from years of the same harness. You trace each one. They hold very still under
your fingers, breathing slowly, and when you reach the bruise they let out a long sigh
and lean back against you.

"You're going to make me wait, aren't you," they murmur.

You turn them around. Their face is flushed, their mouth already parted, and they do not
wait at all: they catch your belt and pull you in, and between kisses they make short
work of every fastening you own, which is somehow far easier going in that direction.

> I take my time with her

You do. You undress her the way you would unwrap something you had waited a long time
for, a piece at a time, kissing each new stretch of skin before you uncover the next.
The inside of her wrist. The hollow of her throat, where her pulse jumps. The curve of
her shoulder, freckled, warm from the fire.

She lies back and lets you. At first she watches, propped on her elbows, a half-smile
playing at her mouth; then her head tips back and her eyes close, and the only sounds
are the small ones she makes, a catch of breath, a soft, wordless hum when your hands
spread over her ribs and slide slowly lower.

There is a scar low on her belly, a neat, old line. You kiss that too. Her fingers come
to rest in your hair, not guiding, just there, stroking absently as you explore, and
when you glance up she is looking at you with an expression you have not seen on her
before, open and unguarded.

"Come here," she says, very quietly.

You go. She draws you down into a long, slow kiss and wraps herself around you, skin to
skin from shoulder to knee, and the unhurried pace you set becomes hers as well: a slow
build, deliberate, every movement drawn out until the waiting itself is half the
pleasure. When it finally breaks over her she holds you tightly and says something
against your shoulder you do not quite catch, and does not let go for a long while after.

> I lie there with him afterwards

The fire has burned down to a red glow. He is sprawled half across you, heavy and loose
limbed, one arm thrown over your waist, his breath slowly evening out against your
collarbone. Your skin is cooling where the blanket does not reach, and neither of you
moves to fix it.

You trace idle shapes on his back. He makes a contented sound, more vibration than
voice, and shifts closer. After a while he lifts his head and props his chin on your
chest to look at you, hair wild, a sleepy, crooked grin on his face.

"I had a speech planned," he says. "Before. I was going to be very charming."

"You were charming."

"I was going to be charming with words." He kisses your sternum, unhurried. "I forgot
all of them the moment you took your boots off."

You laugh, and he feels it, and grins wider. His thumb moves in slow circles at your
hip. The quiet settles back over you both, comfortable, warm in the places you touch,
and somewhere in it his eyes drift closed again, his breathing slowing as sleep takes
him first. You lie awake a little longer, listening to it, feeling his heartbeat
against your ribs, in no hurry at all for the morning.

> I let them lead

not hard, and they follow you down, settling astride your hips with their knees either

"Hands up," they say. You raise them above your head, and they lace their fingers

"Not yet."

They are maddeningly patient. They kiss along your jaw, down your throat, across your

At last they let go of your hands. You reach for them at once, and they let you, sitting

weight and skin, moving with a rhythm they set and you follow. They keep their eyes on
yours the whole way, as though watching you is the point, and when you finally lose
yourself they are right there with you, forehead pressed to yours, breathing your name.
