# Sea hexes: hex travel by ship, by small boat and swimming (feat/sea-hexes, 0.4.9)

The owner, 2026-11-21: "start a lane for 4.9 that deals with the maps for ocean travel so that
we can have the hex travel while on boats or swimming." Added the same day: raise the sea
passage floor from two weeks to three, keep water hexes no quicker than it, and ask World
Bible for longer crossings (ask 35).

## What the code did before

- `Engine._op_hex_move` refused every water hex: "That hex is open water. Crossing it waits
  for a ship." There was no other door onto a water hex.
- **No port touched the sea on the board.** The grid's land is `landAt`'s five discs round
  every town, 70-110 layout units across, and after `Grid._fit` a hex is 3-6 units wide.
  Measured 2026-11-21 with a twelve-hex spiral from each port's own hex: 0 of 23 ports on
  Aurvantis, 0 of 4 on Pangrella, 0 of 7 on the owner's fantasia had a water hex in reach;
  the synthetic world's one port had water eleven hexes off. Putting out from a quay was
  impossible on the map, whatever was built on top.
- Ships, boats and swimming existed off the map: booked passage (`_book_passage`, `_sail`,
  journey segments since 0.4.3), a held ship cruising (`_cruise`), the rowboat or swim out
  to a ship lying off a dockless shore (`_out_to_her`, `_swim_out`, owner ruling
  2026-11-14), the wreck's hours in the water (`_wreck`, `_an_hour_swum`), drowning
  (`breathe`).

## Sources read

- **Ultimate Campaign, Exploration** (aonprd Rules ID 1376, read 2026-11-21). The 12-mile
  hex; Table 3-2 hours a hex by speed. The **water hex** (p.158): "Whether a river, lake, or
  ocean, this type of hex is predominantly water"; without "swim speeds or boats, it is
  best to treat lakes and oceans as obstacles for the PCs to travel around rather than
  through"; difficult water raises the Swim DCs by 5; random encounters "increase by 10%,
  or 25% if the PCs spend most of their time in the water" (p.158, modifying p.155's
  per-hex d% table: 01-50 none, 51-60 hazard, 61-100 monster). UC
  prints no ship speed for a hex and no procedure for sailing one.
- **CRB Swim** (aonprd Skills, read 2026-11-21): half speed as a full-round action on a made
  check; calm 10, rough 15, stormy 20; no taking 10 in stormy water; "Each hour that you
  swim, you must make a DC 20 Swim check or take 1d6 points of nonlethal damage from
  fatigue"; a swim speed moves at that speed without checks, +8, always takes 10.
- **CRB Table 7-9** (already cited in `ships.MILES_A_DAY`): sailing ship 2 mph, 48 miles a
  24-hour day; warship 60, longship 72, galley 96; keelboat 52 as this app reads it.
- **Ultimate Equipment, rowboat** (already cited in `ships.py`): "about 1-1/2 miles per
  hour", two or three Medium passengers; rowboat passage 2 cp a mile.
- **GameMastery Guide Table 7-49** (aonprd Rules ID 850): every ship speed there is "The
  ship's maximum tactical speed in combat" — not a travel speed. The rowboat's 30 ft is
  tactical too. Confirms what `ships.py` already says: the travel rate is Table 7-9.
- **Skull & Shackles** (Paizo forum threads quoting the Player's Guide; the book itself was
  not read, so this is second-hand): the ship "is assumed to sail 24 hours and cover an
  average of 48 miles"; a GM with 30-mile hexes made it three hexes a day. Agrees with
  Table 7-9; not otherwise used.
- **Hexcrawl and seacrawl procedures** (OSR/indie, read 2026-11-21):
  - David Blandy, "making ttrpg procedures" (substack): sea travel in watches, six a day,
    the encounter die at the start of each watch at sea. A pointcrawl of routes, not hexes. Matches this app's watch check
    (`ontheway.WATCH_HOURS`, `SEA_PERCENT`).
  - an itch.io ocean-hex procedure (itch.io/post/10836069): six-mile hexes, one hex a watch
    by ship. A commenter objected that ships and people on foot would then move at the same
    pace (the designer answered that its land hexes are one mile). The objection is the
    shape refused below: one fixed time a hex for every way across the water.
  - GiantITP "Scaling & Pacing a Seacrawl": the same ship speed gives 10, 5 or 3 1/3 hexes a
    day at 12, 24 or 36-mile hexes; the scale problem is the sea's hex size against the
    land's.
  - The Tower of Ascension sailing article (worldanvil.com/w/the-tower-of-ascension-calcas/
    a/sailing-article): a search summary said a ship "may only occupy a hex that has a large
    body of water". **Unconfirmed**: World Anvil refused the fetch (403). Not leaned on: a
    ship entering land only at a port is this app's own rule.
- **VTT**: no Foundry hexcrawl module was found that handles water hexes; the nearest are
  PF2e "Ocean Travel" and Pirate Borg "Sea Travel", both voyage managers (crew, weather,
  days), not hex movers. Could not confirm how Kingmaker's Foundry module treats water.
- **The Alexandrian**: his hexcrawl series checks encounters once a watch; nothing found on
  a naval hexcrawl. Not claimed.

## What was built, and why this way

1. **Ports on the coast** (`Grid._chart_channels`). A town the world puts on the coast
   (`coast` in its near ground: a port, or the export's coast fact) gets a channel of water
   two hexes wide from its gate, straight away from its continent's middle, to where the
   land discs stop, turning in 20-degree steps when it would pass within 30 layout units of
   another town. The town's own hex is never cut. Measured: every port on all four worlds
   has water beside its hex, no town's hex moved, the hex size did not change, and 1,075 of
   Aurvantis's 27,250 region cells (4%) went from land to water (91 of 4,158 on Pangrella).
   This is a stand-in for World Bible's coastline (ask 33); refused: re-laying the land
   (every save's hexes would move), and a "sea gate" that jumps a ship over twenty hexes
   of land drawn between the quay and the water.
2. **Inshore and open water** (`cell["sea"]`): water beside land is the coast's, further out
   is open sea. Drawn apart on the board (a deep-water wash). UC has one water hex; the
   split is this app's, so a boat or a swimmer has a coast to come ashore on and the player
   can see where open sea starts.
3. **Three ways across a water hex, one hex a step, each at the book's own speed**
   (`Engine.water_ways`, `_water_step`):
   - **ship**: a ship the PC holds (`Vessel.held_by`) with her minimum crew
     (`ships.crewed`), at Table 7-9's day's run (six hours a hex for a sailing ship, three
     for a galley). She lies on her hex (`Vessel.hex`); she enters land only at a port,
     where she ties up; beside other land her boat puts the party ashore and she waits,
     and the party can row or swim back out to her.
   - **boat**: a boatman's rowboat hired from a coastal town's hex, UE's passage of 2 cp a
     mile a head and 1-1/2 mph (eight hours and more a hex; CRB Table 7-9 rows it 15
     miles in a ten-hour day, so a hex is inside one rowing day; the UE entry itself could
     not be fetched by the second pass, so its figures stand as `ships.py` cited them on
     2026-11-14). The party is in a `deck` place
     on the hex (no Swim rules until someone goes over the side).
   - **swim**: CRB Swim, round by round through the existing `_swim_out` (take 10 where it
     makes it, the miss-by-5 going under, the breath clock and drowning), the hourly DC 20
     or 1d6 nonlethal through `_an_hour_swum`. Twelve miles at 30 ft is about seven hours
     in the water (15 ft a round, 600 rounds an hour; 7.3 on Pangrella's 12.44-mile hex): a level-one guildhand's Swim fails most of those hourly checks,
     so a swim of one hex costs real damage, and anyone knocked out in the water drowns.
     A swim is **never** chosen for the player and never lit on the board: the refusal and
     the slip both state its hours and its price first. That is how "a swimmer should not
     casually cross open sea" is made apparent: by the rules said up front, not a ban.
   - Refused: one fixed time a hex for every way (the itch.io objection above).
4. **Land and water joins.** Landfall from a boat or the water onto any land hex beside it,
   at that land hex's own Table 3-2 hours; putting out only from land beside the water;
   water reached only from land beside it or from water; walking onto water without a way
   is refused with the three ways and the swim's price named (UC p.158's "obstacle").
5. **The sea's meeting check on every water hex** (`_water_meeting`): the voyage table
   (`ontheway.sea_stretches`) at the cruise's watch rate, 20% a six-hour watch, pro rata for
   the hex's hours. Aboard a held ship it is what any voyage meets (`_met_at_sea`: pirates
   and the navy as ship-to-ship bands, a storm, the wreck one storm in ten). In a rowboat or
   the water, only the owner's 2026-11-21 ruling's aquatic creatures and ships: a creature
   comes or looks, a sail passes; the voyage scenes and storms are a ship's. UC's "+25% in
   the water" was not added: it is a percentage-point bump on UC's own 50% table, and this
   app's base is a different table. Said, not sourced as equal.
6. **No look on water.** A water hex holds no site, so no Perception popup; the water round
   the party clears on the board. A water hex is never `entered`, so it earns no explored
   XP (UC's 100 XP is for exploring land worth exploring; farming it by rowing was refused).

## The sea floor against the hexes (owner call)

The land is made "never quicker than the road" by shrinking the hex (`Grid._fit`). The sea
shares the grid, so it cannot shrink its own hexes without moving the land. **Chosen: the
hours per sea hex stretch** (`Grid.sea_miles`, from the atlas's `sail` miles per crossing):
a water hex is reckoned at the larger of 12 miles and the worst crossing's passage miles
over its straight-line hexes. A ship crosses at least that many hexes, so no crossing by
hexes beats the passage. Measured with the three-week floor: Pangrella 12.44 miles a hex
(81 hexes for 1,008 miles), Aurvantis and fantasia 12 (narrowest crossings 107 and 90
hexes). The floor also now rounds UP (`ships.days_for`): rounded to the nearest, a galley's
10.5 days were 10, 960 miles, quicker than the floor.

## Booked passage (owner call)

**Stays journey segments**, with the ship off the hex map while she is on passage. The
owner's travel-pacing ruling (2026-11-16) is "don't go day by day... a voyage can be a month
long", and a three-week crossing is 84 hexes and more; hex by hex would be the tedium that
ruling removed, and on a berth the crew sails and the party is cargo (nothing to decide per
hex). Hex sailing is for a ship the PC holds. A held ship that starts a voyage or a cruise
leaves the hexes (`Vessel.hex` cleared) and comes back onto them at port. Not built: drawing
a booked ship's position on the board mid-voyage (her place along the line between the two
ports, as `party_hex` does for a halted road). It matters only when a meeting stops a
passage, and at sea the location is the ship, which no nation's map is filed under.

## Found live, 2026-11-21 (port 8975, copies of the owner's data)

Each fixed and pinned in tests/test_sea_hexes.py:

- a party rowed out of Harpost walked its road to Grotburrow from the boat, and the exits row
  offered every road out of town: no road starts on the water now (`_op_journey`, `exits`);
- a swimmer's next click was refused as though standing on the shore: swimming on is the way
  once the swim was chosen;
- the slip said five hours to the owner's undine and the swim took four (`_swim_out` moved a
  swim speed its tactical speed every round): a swim speed crosses at Table 3-2's hours;
- the same undine, out cold from thirst, "swam with ease" hex after hex: a body that is down
  swims nowhere;
- ten hexes under sail left the PC 47 points of thirst damage: a ship feeds and waters her
  people each hex, as `_sail` does a day;
- "the coastal water" read to the narrator as the coast ("you reach the distant shore" a hex
  out from Branford): the name is "the inshore sea" now, and the boat's tell says the party is
  still afloat.

Measured and left as the book has it: nonlethal damage heals 1 an hour a level (CRB), and the
hours of a swim are advanced after its hourly checks, so a level-five swimmer's 13 points from
one hex were healed by the same five hours. A level-one body takes them all at once, which is
the drowning danger; the book interleaves the two and this order is the harsher. Also left:
a rowboat or a swimmer meets only creatures and sails, about 8% a hex against a ship's 17-20%,
because the voyage table's other bands (an island, a stowaway, a storm) are a ship's.
