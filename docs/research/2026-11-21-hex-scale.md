# Hex scale against road time, and walks between a hex and its town (fix/hex-scale)

The owner ruled on 2026-11-21:

1. Walking the hexes between two towns must never be quicker than the road's journey
   floor, on any world.
2. Every walk between a hex site and a town must cost the hexes actually walked, and the
   exits row's price must match what the walk then charges.

## Measured before any change (branch point 64fd12df)

The board's scale is `hexes.Grid.miles_per_unit`: the median, over the world's land roads,
of the road's raw miles divided by its length on the spring layout. A hex was 12 of those
miles. The road journey floors unmeasured roads at 3 days (`journey.ROAD_DAYS_AT_LEAST`),
so a road the world gives as 48 miles was a 3-day journey but only 4 hexes. Walking those 4
hexes on plain took 20 hours, against the road's 24.

Legs where walking the hexes was quicker than the road (plain, 5 hours a hex, 30 ft):

| export | land legs | quicker by hexes | worst leg's shortfall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aurvantis | 152 | 50 | 2.15x |
| Pangrella | 12 | 4 | 1.27x |
| owner's fantasia | 26 | 2 | (not computed) |
| synthetic | 10 | 10 | (not computed) |

Speed was the second hole. `hexes.hours_through` gave every party the 30-foot hours, while
the road's hours scale with speed. So a 20-foot party crossed the board faster than its
road.

Item 2 measured:

- **The passage.** From the ruins (one hex out of Branford), "I head back to Branford's
  docks and book passage" cost 0 minutes. `_book_passage` stands the party on deck from
  wherever it is.
- **The first hex out, from town.** Its doc hangs off the outskirts, so the exits row
  offered it as a ring hop, and the walk charged that: Pangrella's mountain 12 miles out
  was "a few minutes' walk".
- **Correcting my earlier report.** I said the exits row priced "the gate, a quarter of
  an hour, from 12 miles out". On the owner's save that "gate" and "courtyard" are rooms
  of the ruins (`.../the-ruins/the-gate`), not Branford's, so that price was right. Since
  0.4.5, a hex out in the wild lists none of its town's places (`places._out_in_a_hex`).

## Sources

- **Ultimate Campaign, Exploration** (aonprd Rules ID 1376, Table 3-2, read today).
  - Hours to cross a 12-mile hex by party speed: 15 ft 11/16 h, 20 ft 8/12, 30 ft 5/8,
    40 ft 4/6, 50 ft 3/5 (plain / everything else).
  - "set by the slowest member of the group".
  - Difficult terrain counts as one speed category slower.
  - The settlement rules: "Frequently used trails or even simple roads reduce travel time
    through the hex by 25-50%" (legacy.aonprd.com Exploration).
  - So the book has road travel quicker than trackless hexes. Nothing in it says how a hex
    map's scale should be derived.
- **Ultimate Wilderness p.127** (Rules ID 2388): encounter checks only; nothing on scale.
- **The Alexandrian**:
  - The layered hexcrawl, part 2, keeps the road pointmap "in sync" with the hexmap over
    the same region. Resolving road travel through the hex procedure is "quite awkward".
  - His default is the 12-mile hex. For vast empty remixes he grew the hex to 60 miles
    instead of distorting distances.
  - Small hexes "create problems" with spotting distances.
  - The last two points are from search snippets of his posts; I did not read the full
    pages.
  - He does not discuss deriving a board's scale from road times.
- **Kingmaker (tabletop)**, Paizo forum threads only:
  - Restov to Oleg's is about 9 plains hexes on a road/trail, about 90 miles at 12 miles a
    hex, timed with Stolen Lands' travel table.
  - One poster says their group "ignored the scale" of the Brevoy map.
  - Owlcat's CRPG travel numbers could not be sourced.
- **Not sourced.** How Daggerfall, Mount & Blade or Battle Brothers reconcile map scale
  with road speed. I would only be repeating memory, so nothing here rests on them.

What the sources do and do not settle:

- All of them keep **one map at one scale**: the Alexandrian grows the hex rather than
  bending distances.
- All of them make **roads quicker than open country** (UC: 25-50%).
- None of them describe a board whose positions came from a spring layout rather than a
  survey, which is this app's situation (no coordinates in the export; WB ask 33).

## Options

1. **Hours per hex.** Raise the hours until the hexes are slow enough. Rejected: the owner
   ruled the book's 5/8 hours (2026-11-18), and the hours are Table 3-2's.
2. **Miles per hex.** Call a hex fewer than 12 miles. Rejected: the owner ruled 12 miles,
   and so does the book.
3. **The board's scale from the floored road times, uniformly. Chosen.**
   - The hex is shrunk until every land road's own journey hours on foot at 30 ft (from
     `journey.hours_for`, floors and measured roads included) are no more than the hexes
     between its towns times 5 (plain, 30 ft: the quickest any hex goes).
   - One scale for the whole board, as every source keeps.
   - Measured against hours, not miles, so the floor, a world's measured road and slow
     ground are all covered.
   - Only ever smaller, so every old hex maps to its own new one.
   - Table 3-2's ratios between speeds are at least the road's (20 ft: 8/5 = 1.6 against
     the road's 1.5; 40 ft: 4/5 = 0.8 against 0.75; 50 ft: 0.6 against 0.6). So the
     30-foot check holds for every speed once hexes use Table 3-2. That is why Table 3-2 is
     now built.
4. **A road-aware layout.** Give each road's spring its own rest length from its hours,
   so the board's distances follow the roads. Not chosen now: it moves towns, which every
   save stores (`Scene.hexes["layout"]`), and the uniform scale meets the target alone.
   This is listed as an owner call, because the uniform scale is set by the worst leg: on
   Aurvantis one road the layout squeezed 2.15x sets the scale for all of them.
5. **UC's road reduction on road hexes.** Rejected: the road is a separate journey
   (pointcrawl layer), not a line of hexes. That is the Alexandrian's layering, and
   feat/travel-pacing's ruling.

## Saves (`hexes.mend_save`, called from `Campaign.load` before the scene is read)

- Each old hex maps to the new hex its centre falls in.
- Hex place ids are rewritten wherever the scene, the ledger or the model's history holds
  them: the party, everyone's place, founded hexes, sites and their rooms, props, cards,
  quests, the road and the places been. A hex's own head takes the new hex's town and
  ground; a site's head keeps its own ground.
- `touched` and `spotted` are re-keyed.
- Spotted sites are kept as found (`Scene.hexes["sites"]`, read by `site_of`).
- Hex docs are re-made: name, about, terrain and `miles`.
- The party's town becomes the town nearest its new hex.
- The save is stamped `grid: 2`, so a second load changes nothing.
- The turn log and transcript are records and are left as written.

On a copy of the owner's save:

- The party's ruins were 1 hex out of Branford and are now 3 hexes, "the hills 36 miles
  east of Branford".
- The forest the hex was on became hills: the new grid draws ground by rule at the new
  scale.
- Its 10 touched hexes keep their records.
- The ruins are still ruins.
- A second load is identical.

## Item 2 (`outskirts._hex_hop`, `Engine.back_from_the_hex`)

- **A step between a hex and its town's own places** costs the hexes between them at
  Table 3-2's hours (the hex doc now carries `miles` from town). It is the one function
  both the exits row and the walk call (`hop_minutes`), so the row's price is the walk's.
- **A hex's miles do not make it a "reach of the hinterland".** That happened in the live
  check: the ruins' way up to their own hex read "more than a day", and is fixed and
  tested.
- **A passage booked from open ground first walks the hexes back** on the straight line
  to the town's hex, each at its hours, through the body's toll and one road check per
  hex, as `_op_hex_move` charges them. A meeting stops it on that hex and the booking
  waits.
