# Research note, lane fix/hex-sites (playtest 2026-11-20 items 1 and 2)

Searched 2026-11-20. A second pass checked the claims below against the sources before
any code was written (see "Checked" at the end).

## Item 1: a hex site was an empty two-room venture

**What happened.** `_op_hex_enter` goes through the venture door (`_op_venture`). For the
ruins that door mints a head and two or three seeded spots ("the gate", "the courtyard"),
then rolls 50% for an inhabitant and `dungeons.chance` (20% base) for a way down. In the
owner's save both rolls missed. The head record has no `deeper`, so there was nothing to
find and every search came back empty. And even when the way down does roll, its feature
sits in a child spot ("the undercroft", or the last spot minted) that the player never
walks to: they arrive at the head and search there.

**Sources.**
- Pathfinder RPG Ultimate Campaign, "Exploration" (legacy PRD,
  https://legacy.aonprd.com/ultimateCampaign/campaignSystems/exploration.html). It
  defines landmark and hidden sites. It says "the answer should never be 'nothing'" and
  gives 100 XP for a fully explored hex. Its hidden-site examples have their own DCs
  (hill caverns "DC 10 Perception or Survival"; mountain paths DC 20 Perception, which only waives the extra travel time); the 100 XP gains 25 more for difficult terrain or a hazard. It describes sites
  as encounters, lairs or ruins that "could still contain lost treasures". (The PRD page
  does not call one site type "standard"; it uses that word only for a hex without a
  terrain element.)
- The Alexandrian, "Hexcrawl" series, on keying the map
  ("Hexcrawl – Part 4: Encounter Tables", https://thealexandrian.net/?p=17333): "Most hexes have only a single keyed
  location". Hidden locations need a second check, and "if an encounter is not indicated,
  the location has not actually been found". The post does not say whether a site's
  contents stay the same on a return visit (only a commenter does), so that point is
  unsourced here.
- Pathfinder Adventure Path *Kingmaker*: the Stolen Lands hexes are keyed by hand, with
  authored lairs and camps. I could not get a primary page for any one hex's keyed
  contents (only forum threads and campaign logs, e.g. https://paizo.com/threads/rzs2lj4o),
  so I take nothing from it beyond the shape: a keyed site is written content, not an
  empty map marker.
- Daggerfall (UESP, https://dev.uesp.net/wiki/Daggerfall:Dungeons, read through search
  snippets and its pt.uesp.net mirror, since the page returned 403). Its dungeons are
  built from a fixed set of prebuilt blocks. Their layouts "were hard-coded in the release
  media and thus are never-changing", and UESP says they were probably made with a
  pseudo-random program. The main quest dungeons were built by hand, and a forum user says
  monsters and treasure are randomised, so the contents do not stay the same. Players complained
  that the dungeons all look alike, and one design critique called them "meaningless
  exploration". Those complaints come from Steam, GOG and review pages found by search,
  with no single URL kept, so they are unverified. What
  was abandoned: generated filler with no story. The developer's sources could not be
  found; those are fan and review sources.
- The project's own rules/dungeons.py header already records B/X stocking, the Five Room
  Dungeon, cyclic loops, and the WWN ruin tag with layered history (searched 2026-11-16).
  Those are the dungeon builder this fix reuses.

**Chosen.** A hex site is the dungeon the existing builder makes
(`content/dungeons/*.json` concepts, `dungeons.generate`). There is no second builder.
`dungeons.on_entering(..., site=True)` always records the way down for the site the
world put in that hex (`Engine._is_hex_site`: `hexes.site_of` names this kind for this
hex). The feature goes at the site's own head, where the party arrives (`dungeons.arrived`
makes the reactive look there) and searches (`dungeons.searched`). On a miss, the feature's
sign is shown ("the rubble lies oddly here…"). A search, or taking 20, at the CRB
secret-door DC 20 finds the way down. That mints 4-6 rooms with their concept's
inhabitants, traces, traps, treasure, and the quest. Everything is seeded off the head id,
so every visit, in every campaign, gets the same site. `on_entering` now runs before the
walk in, so the record exists when the arrival look is made.

**Rejected.**
- A second hex-site builder (own room table). It duplicates `generate` and drifts from it.
- Raising `dungeons.chance` for every venture. Then every cave the player goes looking for
  on ordinary ground becomes a dungeon, which is Daggerfall's filler problem. The guarantee
  applies to the world's keyed site only.
- Putting the way down in plain sight (find DC 0) at every site. UC and the Alexandrian
  both keep hidden content behind a check. The CRB DC 20 with a visible sign keeps what is
  found earned, and taking 20 always reaches it.
- Making the existing child spots (gate, courtyard) into stocked rooms. That would be a
  second stocking path; the dungeon rooms already are the site.

## Item 2: a rumoured dungeon could not be found at its landmark

**What happened.** The carter's line was recorded as a heard place, "the old mine", kind
"mine head", with landmark the woods. The town's rumour card (`dungeons.spread_rumours`)
is for the venture head `~underground:the-woods/the-old-mine`. #28 was read as `search`
with object "the cave everyone has a story about", place "the woods", and no `where`. That
defaults to "elsewhere", which builds no op, so the planner rolled tracks and a
secret-door check. The dungeon-quest ruling (owner, 2026-11-16) expects the way down to be
found by Perception at its spot inside the venture. Nothing led from the woods into the
venture. "I go to the old mine" was worse: `go_to_heard_place` founded a `mine head`
building called the old mine beside the woods (`found`, a different id), so there was no
way down at all.

**Sources.**
- UC Exploration (above). A hidden site needs "an appropriate skill check" while
  exploring, at the site's own DC. The project already holds these DCs as `hexes.FIND_DC`
  (tower 10, ruins 12, mine 15, cave 18); a kind without one uses UC's own easiest
  example, DC 10.
- The Alexandrian, "Hexcrawl" (above). The second check finds the hidden location; a miss
  means it "has not actually been found". This matches the CRB p.564 rule the engine's
  search already follows: a miss says nothing.
- Morrowind and Skyrim directions-to-a-landmark, and Inform's *familiar* state, are already
  cited in rules/heard_places.py. A heard place is known about but not found, and its
  landmark is where to look.
- The Alexandrian, "Rumor tables, part 2" (cited in rules/dungeons.py). A rumour is a
  hook to a real place, so the place it names has to be reachable from it.

**Chosen.**
- `heard_places.ventures_at(scene, here)` lists every heard place still to be found whose
  landmark is here and which is a venture. Its kind comes from its name if that is a
  venture's own label, else from the town's open rumour card for a venture off that
  landmark. Both are structural comparisons, never a reading of the player's English.
  Every heard place at the landmark is looked for, so "the cave" finds the one old mine
  heard of there.
- `acts_to_ops`: a `search` builds the engine's search when a heard way in has its
  landmark where the party stands, whatever `where` the reader chose. The reader's schema
  is unchanged.
- `Engine._search_for_heard`: the one search roll against the kind's DC. If it beats the
  DC, the party goes in by the one venture door, onto the rumour's seeded head, so its way
  down and the quest are there.
- `go_to_heard_place` (and the act table's ownership): going to a heard way in is a
  `venture` off its landmark, never a `found`.

**Rejected.**
- Matching the player's word to the record ("cave" against "mine"). That is regex reading
  English, which the owner ruled out (docs/structured-turn.md), and it fails exactly here.
- A new reader slot ("searching for a heard place"). The landmark plus the record already
  decide it mechanically, so a slot would add a model answer where code is certain.
- Founding the venture head without moving the party, for a later `travel`. `travel` to
  the head skips the venture door, so the inhabitant roll and `on_entering` (the way down)
  would never run, and the dungeon would be lost.
- A separate Survival roll. UC allows Perception or Survival. The search already rolls
  Perception once, and one roll per search is the engine's rule.

## Item 19 (owner ruling, 2026-11-20): a search looks for what it names

**What happened.** The reader's `search` act has two closed answers, `where` (here or
elsewhere) and `take`. Only "here" or a take built the engine's search. Everything else
owed no op, so the planner filled the turn with whatever it liked. In #28 that was a
`track` (Survival for footprints) and a Perception check for secret doors, and the page
answered about tracks. When the engine's search did run, it set the roll against hidden
creatures, ways down and traps, and on a miss it said "finds nothing", whatever the player
had been looking for.

**Sources.**
- PF1e Perception (CRB p.102, https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/perception/): "Most
  Perception checks are reactive, made in response to observable stimulus.
  Intentionally searching for stimulus is a move action." A search is a look for
  something, at that thing's own DC.
- PF1e Survival (https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/survival/): tracks are Survival, not
  Perception ("To find tracks or to follow them … requires a successful Survival
  check"). Food and water in the wild are Survival too ("get along in the wild", DC 10).
  So "no tracks" is never the answer to a Perception search, and water found out in the
  wild belongs to the live-off-the-land door, not the search.
- Inform 7 Standard Rules (https://ganelson.github.io/inform/standard_rules/actns.html,
  and search summaries of older 6M62 pages): `searching` applies to a named thing
  ("search [something]"), and by default only containers and supporters can be searched.
  An author adds rules for other things. The object of the search decides the answer.
  I could not confirm whether there is a built-in "look for" action, so nothing is taken
  from that.
- MUDs: Diku-family "search" commands are commonly a hunt for hidden exits. I could not
  find a primary source for this, so it is not relied on. That habit is the failure this
  ruling forbids.

**Chosen.**
- The reader gets a third closed answer on `search`, `seeking` (`interpret.SEEKING`):
  anything, a person, a place, a thing, or water. It is demonstrated on the
  ledger's line only, because the reader's prompt budget had no room for more (measured:
  49,849 characters against 49,228 with three demonstrations). Water is the one kind no engine finder can ground from the words.
  The others are answered by the engine's own finders whatever the reader said, and the
  reader's kind only decides which finder is asked first.
- `acts_to_ops`: every search that names an object builds the engine's search, with
  `sought` (the player's words) and `seeking`. The plan's own track, Survival or forage
  for it is replaced, as the existing `apply` rule already does for a built search.
- `Engine._sought` / `_sought_line` answer about the thing. A person is checked with
  `scope.in_the_room`: they are here, or they are hidden and not found. A thing is a
  prop here or carried, matched with `holding.same`. A place uses `places.find`: you are
  at it, it is close by (an exit), or it is not here. Water is here, a step away, or the
  town's free water; otherwise none, with the live-off-the-land door named out in the
  wild. If nothing on the record answers to the words and nothing hidden was found, the
  page says the player "does not find" that thing, by name.
- A river or spring sought out in the wild stays the reader's `fend`. That is the owner's
  2026-11-17 #6 demonstration, and the book's way to water (Survival, get along in the
  wild), which waters the party. Measured live on this branch: "I search the woods for a
  river" was read `fend` and came back with four hours foraging and a day's food and
  water, with no tracks. `seeking: water` covers a search the reader files as a search
  (the town's well).
- Ways down and traps are looked for only when the words name nothing the record holds
  (a hatch, a way down, the gallery) or name nothing at all. Hidden creatures are still
  noticed on the roll, as a reactive check (CRB p.102).

**Rejected.**
- Matching words like "river", "track" or "door" in the player's line: regex reading
  English, ruled out.
- An enum value per finder with no fallback. A small reader misfiling "the old woman" as
  a thing would then miss her, so the finders are all asked.
- Running the ways-down search on every search. That is the very thing the owner ruled
  against ("searching for that thing, not tracks").

## Owner calls made provisionally
- The search that finds a heard way in also goes in (2 hours, like any venture). Finding
  and entering are not separate turns.
- The DC for a heard way in is the kind's hex-site DC, with no cover bonus. You were told
  where it is, which is taken to offset the cover.
- A search for something named does not look for ways down or traps. Somebody hidden is
  still noticed on the roll.
- A search for water out in the wild names the live-off-the-land door rather than rolling
  Survival itself.
