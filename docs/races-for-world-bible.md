# Race cards: what Pathfinder GM needs to receive a playable race

**Written 2026-09-10, after looking at a live Vanara card in the World Bible race
editor.** Companion to `docs/for-world-bible.md`, which lists everything we want; this is
ask #1 in detail, because it shipped and it does not yet arrive playable.

Four files go together. Hand over all four:

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `races-for-world-bible.md` | this document — the diagnosis, the rules, the worked rewrite |
| `race-options.md` | **the complete list** of every option a card has. Generated from the consumer's own table |
| `race-cues.json` | the same list, machine-readable |
| `check_race_cards.py` | a standalone checker. Run it on an export before shipping |

`race-options.md` and `race-cues.json` are generated by
`tools/export_race_cues.py` in the Pathfinder GM repo and must be regenerated when the
consumer's cue table changes. Do not edit them by hand.

---

## The short answer

The instinct behind "I don't believe this will ship ready for play" is right, and there
are **two separate reasons**. One is fixable by writing better cards. The other is not
fixable by writing at all, and needs a format change.

### Reason one: the cards are thin, and they duplicate themselves

The Vanara card, run through the consumer:

```
728 characters of prose across 5 lines
  →  size medium, speed 30
  →  2 traits: low-light vision, climb 20 ft
  →  1 of those 2 does anything in play (the engine has no walls to climb)
```

The same run on the Korvu card in the **shipped 1.1 export**:

```
  →  3 traits: fly 30 ft, blindsense 30 ft, claws
  →  1 of those 3 does anything in play
  →  and the same sentence appears in both `body` and `movement`
```

Both cards put **the identical sentence in `body` and in `movement`**. That is not an
authoring slip in one card; it is the generator writing the same paragraph into two
fields. It costs nothing mechanically — see "How the consumer actually reads a card"
below — but it means the `movement` field is doing no work, and a reader looking at the
editor sees an obviously broken record.

### Reason two: the format has a hard ceiling

This is the important one.

**A race card can express a size, a speed, and up to twelve traits. That is the whole of
it.** There is no channel for anything else, and no wording reaches one. In particular a
card cannot say:

- **any ability score modifier** — the single most defining mechanical feature of a
  Pathfinder race. Every world race is currently given the generic
  +2 physical / +2 mental / −2 any and chooses at the table
- any skill bonus, save bonus, or conditional modifier
- creature type — every world race is `humanoid`
- bonus feats or extra skill ranks
- languages beyond the people's own

And of the twelve traits, **six do nothing in play yet** — fly, swim, climb and burrow
have no engine to move through, and claws and bite are not rolled because the engine
rolls the weapon in hand.

So a *perfectly written* card today yields, at best, a size, a speed, and a handful of
senses. That is a flavourful race and not yet a playable one. Fixing the prose is worth
doing and will not, on its own, get there. **The format needs one more field** — the
proposal is at the bottom.

## How the consumer actually reads a card

Worth knowing before writing any rules about the fields, because it is not what the
editor's labels imply.

`rules/races.py:from_world` takes `body[]`, `senses[]` and `movement[]`, **joins all
three into one block of text**, lower-cases it, and runs twelve regular expressions over
the result. Whichever match, grant their traits. Then `size` and `speed` are read from
their own fields, and `about` is kept as description.

Three consequences, all of which matter:

1. **Which field a word sits in makes no difference.** "They climb" in `body` and "They
   climb" in `movement` fire the same single cue. The fields are for the human reading
   the editor, not for the matcher.
2. **Saying a thing twice does nothing.** A cue fires once. Duplication is pure waste.
3. **Everything that matches nothing is discarded.** Sociology, history, dress, labour,
   settlement patterns — all read, all matched, all dropped. They are not wrong to write
   in `about`; they are wasted in `body`, `senses` and `movement`.

The whole vocabulary is in `race-options.md`.

## The rules for a card

### `size` — a word

`tiny`, `small`, `medium` or `large`. Never a modifier, never a height in feet.
Large is recognised and **not granted**: the Advanced Race Guide allows Large for giants
only, so a towering people is recorded medium with a note explaining why.

### `speed` — a word

`slow`, `normal` or `fast`. These become 20, 30 and 40 feet on the consumer's side.
Never write the number.

### `body[]`, `senses[]`, `movement[]` — one plain sentence per line

Five rules, in the order they are broken:

1. **One sentence per line.** Not a paragraph. The Vanara card's second `body` line is
   34 words carrying one cue word; the first line is 9 words carrying the same one.
2. **Say the thing, then stop.** "They climb sheer trunks without rope." is complete.
   Everything after "a trait that shapes everything from their traditional dress to the
   kind of labour they're steered toward from childhood" belongs in `about`.
3. **Never repeat a sentence between fields.** Put the body in `body`, how they move in
   `movement`, what they perceive in `senses`. If a people's defining trait is climbing,
   the *tail and shoulders* are body and the *climbing* is movement — the same fact, said
   from the angle each field is asking about.
4. **Use the world's words, not Pathfinder's.** The Vanara card says "Low-light vision",
   which is a rules term. It happens to work, because "low-light" is one of the trigger
   words — but it works **by accident**, and the next card that says it properly ("they
   see clearly by dusk light") is the one that proves the point. Write what is true about
   the people; the consumer owns the rules name.
5. **No digits.** Not a range in feet, not a number of limbs, not a lifespan. The
   consumer supplies every number.

### `about` — one paragraph, and the place for everything else

This is shown on the character-creation screen and is **not** matched against anything.
All the culture, history, homeland, labour and dress that does not belong in the three
sentence fields belongs here. It is the only field where length is a virtue.

## The Vanara card, rewritten

### As it stands

```json
{
  "id": "vanara", "name": "Vanara", "people_id": "vanara",
  "size": "medium", "speed": "normal",
  "body": [
    "Simian features, a fully prehensile tail, exceptional climbing ability.",
    "Vanara are physically defined by their simian features, a fully prehensile tail, exceptional climbing ability, a trait that shapes everything from their traditional dress to the kind of labor they're steered toward from childhood."
  ],
  "senses": [
    "Low-light vision, a trait that shows up constantly in how Vanara communities plan settlements, stand watch, and work at night."
  ],
  "movement": [
    "Simian features, a fully prehensile tail, exceptional climbing ability.",
    "Vanara are physically defined by their simian features, a fully prehensile tail, exceptional climbing ability, a trait that shapes everything from their traditional dress to the kind of labor they're steered toward from childhood."
  ],
  "about": "Vanara trace their homeland to veyl isles' canopy settlements..."
}
```

→ **2 traits, 1 usable, 728 characters spent, `movement` duplicating `body`.**

### As it should be

```json
{
  "id": "vanara", "name": "Vanara", "people_id": "vanara",
  "size": "medium", "speed": "fast",
  "body": [
    "Simian in build, long-limbed and light through the shoulder.",
    "A fully prehensile tail, strong enough to take their whole weight.",
    "Their hands grip well and hold a tool poorly."
  ],
  "senses": [
    "They see clearly by dusk light and keep working in it."
  ],
  "movement": [
    "They climb sheer trunks and rope without slowing.",
    "They move through the canopy faster than they cross open ground."
  ],
  "about": "Vanara trace their homeland to the canopy settlements of the Veyl Isles, built high in the island forests, and even where they have spread across Aurvantis that origin still shapes their architecture, their diet and their holidays. Troop-based extended families, led by whichever elder currently commands the most respect. Their livelihoods are shaped by the Veyl Isles' coral shallows, mangrove wetland and high island canopy, and their traditional trades reflect the habitats that shaped them."
}
```

Measured, not asserted — this is the checker's own output on that card:

```
builds: size medium, speed fast, 2 trait(s), 1 usable in play
  [ok  ] low-light vision
  [wait] climb 20 ft
note     body triggers no cue — its words reach nothing
338 characters of prose (was 728)
```

**Read that last note carefully, because it is the whole argument.** The rewrite is
clean: no duplication, each field answering its own question, half the prose, culture
moved to `about` where the player actually sees it, and the speed now correctly `fast`.
And it grants *exactly the same two traits as the broken version*, one of which the
engine cannot use.

The entire `body` field — simian build, a prehensile tail strong enough to hang from,
hands that grip well and hold a tool poorly — reaches nothing at all. Those are three
true, vivid, well-written sentences about what a Vanara *is*, and there is no row in the
cue table for a tail.

That is the ceiling. It is not the writing, and no amount of rewriting moves it.

## Check before shipping

```bash
python check_race_cards.py <world>-campaign.json
```

Put `race-cues.json` beside the script. It reports, per card, exactly which traits
Pathfinder GM would build, how many of them work in play, and every way the card wastes
its words — duplication between fields, Pathfinder vocabulary, digits, 30-word sentences,
and fields that trigger nothing at all.

It exits non-zero only on real problems (a missing id, a bad size, a card that fires no
cue whatsoever). Notes are advice. The summary counts three things separately, because
"clean" was the wrong headline: a card can pass every check, have no problems, and still
be too thin to play.

Run it against the current 1.1 export and you will get the Korvu report quoted at the top
of this document.

## The proposal: one more field, and races become playable

To close reason two without breaking the boundary rule — **World Bible supplies words,
Pathfinder GM prices them** — the card needs a way to say what a people is *good and bad
at*, in words a world can honestly know about itself.

Add two fields:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `strengths[]` | up to two words from the list below |
| `weakness` | one word from the same list, or empty |

with a small, fixed, deliberately rules-free vocabulary:

```
strong        powerful of build
nimble        quick and well-balanced
hardy         resilient, hard to wear down
clever        quick to learn and reason
perceptive    attentive, hard to surprise
commanding    forceful of personality
```

Not one of those six words is a Pathfinder term, and any world can say them about its
peoples without knowing a rules system exists. The consumer maps them onto the Advanced
Race Guide's standard +2 / +2 / −2 array and prices the result, exactly as it already
maps "wings" onto a flight speed at 4 race points.

For the Vanara that would be:

```json
"strengths": ["nimble", "perceptive"],
"weakness": "commanding"
```

which is a recognisable Vanara — and a *playable* one, because the sheet finally differs
from every other race in the world in the way a Pathfinder race is supposed to differ.

**This needs agreeing before it is built**, on both sides: the consumer has to read the
new fields, and this document should be updated with the outcome. It is additive, so it
is a MINOR schema bump (1.1 → 1.2) and no existing consumer breaks.

A second, optional field worth discussing at the same time — `knacks[]`, a handful of
words for what a people are practised at ("climbing", "swimming", "tracking", "trading",
"stonework") which the consumer maps to skill bonuses. Lower value than `strengths[]`,
and the same shape of decision.

## Summary for whoever picks this up

1. **Fix the generator so `movement` stops duplicating `body`.** Both shipped cards do
   it. This is the one unambiguous bug.
2. **Split the prose:** one sentence per line, the sentence that names the trait and
   nothing after it, culture moved to `about`.
3. **Stop writing Pathfinder terms** in the sentence fields. "Low-light vision" is ours,
   not the world's.
4. **Read `race-options.md`** while writing. It is the complete list of what a card can
   trigger; there is nothing else.
5. **Run `check_race_cards.py`** on the export before shipping it.
6. **Raise the `strengths[]` / `weakness` proposal** with the Pathfinder GM side. Steps
   1–5 make the cards clean and honest; step 6 is what makes a race playable.
