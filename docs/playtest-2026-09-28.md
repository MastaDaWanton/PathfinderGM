# Playtest 2026-09-28 — master 5cabed1, the Bobby campaign

Played on a test server of local master 5cabed1 (every branch merged by hand that morning),
against the player's real data, in Aurvantis. One character — Bobby, an asura wizard 1 with
the Bonesetter background — from the opening in Vormoor, through the market, out of the gate
and into the forest, to a Burning Hands cast.

**Instruction: catalogue and investigate only; no code changed.** Every cause below was
measured on the save (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\PathfinderGM\campaigns\bobby.json` and its `.1`–`.3`
backups), the running server's `/api/state`, the fixture, or the code — not guessed. Where
something could not be confirmed it says so.

Two lenses apply to every correction here, both standing instructions:

- **World-agnostic.** Aurvantis is one export with its own habits (every settlement
  `kind: CITY`, every cast role "Person", scale-blind stock sentences, no compass bearings).
  A fix keyed on its names, ids or fact labels is not a fix. Every correction is tested
  against a second world or a synthetic one with different fact keys.
- **The export is part of the fix.** Where a correction needs data World Bible does not ship,
  or ships in a poor shape, build the fallback here AND record the wanted field and its best
  format in `docs/from-world-bible.md` / `docs/campaign-format.md`, so a newly generated world
  is playable on arrival. The collected asks are in the last section.

And the third, from CLAUDE.md: **research how it was already solved before designing any
group** — look hardest for what the traditions tried and abandoned. Suggested sources are
noted per group; none of that research has been done yet.

The asura is a **test race** (homebrew, usable only under the unlimited-RP house rule): its
Str 36 / Dex 60 / AC 37 are expected and are not an item.

## Items, in the order they were reported

### 1. The opening calls Vormoor a sprawling city; the panel says VILLAGE

"Vormoor rises from the water… a sprawling settlement… the city's bustling thoroughfares",
under a panel reading VORMOOR · VILLAGE.

**Cause.** The app is right and the world contradicts itself:
- `scale: "village"`, summary "Village", History "grown into a village" — the structured
  scale. `places.scale_of` reads it; panel, brief and template all say village.
- `kind: CITY` on every settlement (World Bible's word for "settlement"); path `…/cities/…`.
- World Bible's settlement templates are scale-blind: **all 64** Aurvantis settlements say
  "drawn… from the city's own leading families" — 48 of them are not cities. Vormoor also
  carries "the city's trade", a fact keyed **Urban Life**, and "the region's acknowledged
  center of learning".
- `play/opening_prose.material` hands the model the facts and the place's own writing with
  **no scale line**; the scale appears only inside "The template says:", the text the model
  is told to rewrite.
- The worked EXAMPLE's material also has no scale line, yet its answer opens "Hollin Stair is
  a fishing town" — it demonstrates *inferring* the size from facts.
- `opening_prose.problems` has no scale check, so the draft shipped.

**Correction.**
1. A "What kind of place (fact): a village of a few hundred people…" line in the material,
   from `places.what_it_is` (the one composer the panel and brief use); the same line in the
   EXAMPLE so it demonstrates copying the scale, not inferring it.
2. Normalise the world's stock scale words before any model sees them: a helper in
   `rules/places.py` that rewrites "the city"/"the city's" to the settlement's scale word
   (never "the city of X", another place), used by the opening material and by
   `play/gm_search`; show the "Urban Life" key as "Daily life".
3. A size check in `problems()`: in a village or town, *city, sprawling, metropolis, tens of
   thousands, districts, thoroughfares, crowds* → named for the rewrite; hard, so a draft
   still wrong falls to the template. Test records this draft.
4. Measure the turn narrator (town audit script in a village) before adding the same check
   to `narration.review`.

**Export.** Scale-aware settlement wording; `kind` documented as "settlement" (or a real
kind per scale).

### 2. Every campaign starts in Vormoor

**Cause.** `opening.starting_place` (play/opening.py:200) ranks settlements by
(facts, sections, summary length) then name, reversed; no seed, no character. Every Aurvantis
settlement has identical fact and section counts (templated), so it falls to summary length —
"Village" (7) beats "Town"/"City" (4) — and then reverse-alphabetical: **Vormoor** is the last
of the 16 villages. "The best-described place" has become "the alphabetically last village".

**Correction.** Start town chosen with the campaign's own seed among the adequately described
settlements, weighted by the character (their people's lands, background ties); optionally
chosen on the new-campaign screen.

### 3. The twelve starts are one start

"How many starts are there… never starts me in a tavern or just on the road… the entry point
matters."

**Cause.**
- **12** situations (`opening.SITUATIONS`), rolled `1d12` seeded by `_seed_from(campaign_id)`
  — and the campaign id is the **character's name slugified**, so a name always gets the same
  start (masta → the step in the sun, john → the work yard, dorito → the public square).
  Across 12,000 random names the twelve come up evenly (962–1,045), so it is not a weighting
  bug; it is identity.
- All twelve edges are one idea: *something stopped / went quiet and nobody says why*. The
  shape of the table is the shape of the output.
- No start puts anything in engine state (no fight, no patient, no offer); the first turn can
  only ask "what's going on?".
- All twelve errands are the same broke stranger (sell, eat, bed, work).
- The background never shapes the start: `_bind_background` runs after the roll and only adds
  ties, and `acquaint` re-labels whoever was already rolled.
- No road start (#5 is already at the gate); no start says tavern or inn.

**Correction.**
1. Roll starts from a fresh per-campaign seed, stored with the campaign.
2. Starts become documents (like backgrounds): which backgrounds/places they suit, where
   (in town / a tavern / the road outside), the incident in motion, who leads you in and what
   they say, and the **hand-off** moment the player takes over (the player's example: the
   cage-fight owner walking the bonesetter to the patient, talking as he goes, stopping where
   the Heal check begins).
3. Engine-backed: the caravan attack is a real encounter placed at level; the cage fight
   leaves a real wounded fighter.
4. Road starts (needs item 17's outskirts).
5. Background-weighted, generic fallback, varied incident *kinds* (attack, injury, offer,
   chase, fire). Of the 14 backgrounds at least seven suggest their own start (bonesetter,
   pit fighter, caravan hand, ferryman, gate watch, innkeeper's child, thief-taker).

**Research first.** Dragon Age: Origins (one prologue per origin); Dungeon World and Blades
in the Dark first-session / in-medias-res rules; what they abandoned.

### 4. The watchman was described on the third beat of talking to him

"The watchman waving traffic through is an Orc: old enough to have stopped counting…" spliced
into turn 3.

**Cause.** The face backstop (`judgement.settle_descriptions`, play/views.py:2125) detects a
mention by the **last word of the name** (`_mentions`, gm/judgement.py:6490). His name is the
opening's role phrase verbatim — "the watchman waving traffic through" — so the key is
**"through"**. Beats 1–2 said "the watchman" and never "through": not mentioned, nothing owed.
Beat 3 said "the way **through** the first cluster of houses": mentioned, faceless, owed. The
opening itself never runs the check, so the opening companion is never described at first
sight.

**Correction.** Mention = the name or its head noun, never its last word; run the check on the
opening; a role phrase is not a name (item 4b).

**4b. A role phrase is not a person's name.** The panel and the face line both read "the
watchman waving traffic through". The opening's `who` should give a short label ("the
watchman") with the phrase kept as description.

### 5. "I ask him about the girl in the market" spawned a girl beside me

**Cause.** `judgement.inject_company` (gm/judgement.py:5820): `_ADDRESSES` matches "I **ask**
…" and takes the rest — "him about the girl in the market" — as the addressee; `_CIVILIANS`
finds "girl"; a guildhand named "girl" is spawned **engaged** at the gate while the watchman
says "She's in the market, near the well". The interpreter read it right (`talk`, target
"him") and was not consulted. The spawn: true name "Korvin Korvath", pronouns they/them, race
human, appearance Orc, a club.

**Correction.**
1. Asking *about* someone is not addressing them — gate `inject_company` on the interpreter's
   target, or at minimum stop at "him/her/them about". One of the regex detectors the
   interpreter exists to retire.
2. A person mentioned as elsewhere exists elsewhere: a population record placed at the market,
   found there on arrival (the finder and residency already hold people elsewhere).
3. A spawn honours the player's word: "girl" means she/her, young, and a name and people to
   match.

### 6. The watchman's line was booked as Bobby's

The plan's `say` carried "She doesn't like the desperate ones…" with because "the player's
words commit the turn to it"; the tell reads "Bobby speaks, to the effect that…". Prose
attributed it correctly by luck; the engine's record did not.

**Correction.** A `say` whose words are not in the player's input is never the PC's.

### 7. A conversation log, and a sidebar that is panels

"It should pop out with a log of what has been said… just the dialogue and vocalizations such
as grunting or laughing… multiple panels, one of those should be the conversation tab."

**What exists.** The desktop `<aside>` is one fixed column (sheet, map, In the scene,
conversation, Rolls); under 760px it is already a slide-over drawer from an edge tab. Every
spoken line is already tagged `<say who=… to=…>` and lifted into who/to/line records
(gm/speech.py:208) — kept per beat, then dropped. Vocalisations are untagged prose.

**Correction.**
1. Per-person conversation log, persisted: attributed lines both ways, vocalisations in
   italics, no scenery; reopens with the history. Feeds "since last we met" and memory.
2. Vocalisations captured — a tag, or detection (known speaker + vocal verb in one sentence)
   with a repair; either way a fallback for untagged beats (measured 2026-09-25: tagging was
   all-or-nothing per beat, 4 of 8).
3. The sidebar as closeable tabbed panels — Scene, Conversation, Map, Rolls, Sheet —
   collapsible to edge tabs on desktop like the phone drawer; Conversation comes forward when
   someone starts talking; one panel system for both widths.

**Research first.** Foundry VTT's sidebar (tabbed, collapsible, pop-out tabs), BG3 and Disco
Elysium dialogue histories.

### 8. Drenn Ironvale is the first named person in every playthrough

**Cause.** Three systems take **the first of Vormoor's four cast members** (Drenn Ironvale,
Korgath Varn, Mokra Sootspar, Zasha Sootspar), and every campaign starts in Vormoor:
1. **Background ties.** `backgrounds.bind` → `schemes._role_for` (rules/schemes.py:494)
   matches the tie's role against the cast list's `role` — which is **"Person"** for every
   member (their own entities say otherwise: Drenn's is "Role: healer"). Nothing matches; the
   deterministic fallback `cast[len(taken) % len(cast)]` gives the first tie cast[0].
2. **The starting quest.** "The lost thing" opens `at($market)` from hour zero in every
   campaign; its giver slot goes through the same `_role_for` with its own `taken` set → Drenn
   again ("Find Drenn Ironvale's lost Power leaf", c4).
3. **Keeper names.** `keepers.name_for` (rules/keepers.py:114) builds a keeper's name from a
   world given name + a **family taken from the town's cast** (Ironvale/Varn/Sootspar), seeded
   by place id — so the market is always kept by **Ashla Ironvale**, "one of the Ironvales,
   and so is Drenn". The world's own 16 Vormoor family names (Grimstone, Fellhaven…) are
   unread by this path.

**Correction.**
1. Roles from the person's own entity when the cast says "Person".
2. Background and scheme slots pick among qualifying people with the **campaign** seed (a
   place keeping its keeper within a campaign is fine).
3. One `taken` set across binding and schemes — your past and your first quest-giver are not
   the same man by accident.
4. Keepers draw families from the world's own `play.names` for the town as well as the cast.
5. "The lost thing" at hour zero in every game belongs with the starts rework (item 3).

**Export.** Real roles on `play.cast`.

### 9. The market's keeper should not be the first person you meet; the quest took over the search

"I head to the market and look for the girl…" → the girl did not exist there

Ironvale's lost Power leaf") made Drenn walk up and deliver his quest; the search was dropped.

3. Item 5.2 (the mentioned person exists where mentioned).

### 10. A market is a crowd, and its master is busy

"Its keeper would no doubt be swamped… she would hardly have the time to be meeting with the
user when they are basically a nobody."

**Cause.** `places.STAFFED["the market"]` (rules/places.py:277) gives the market ONE person,
"the stallholder who runs the pitch" — both the market's authority and the only seller the
trade panel can use.

**Correction.**
1. Stallholders are who you trade with: stalls by goods line (`goods_at(kind)` already groups
   them); "buy a coil of rope" finds the cord stall's trader, minted on first need and kept
   (residency's idiom); anonymous until dealt with.
2. The market master is an authority, not a shop — pitch fees, measures, disputes; busy; a
   nobody is brushed off; regard, a background tie (Stallholder, Guild clerk) or a matter
   they care about earns a hearing (reuse the knock rules of `call_on`).
3. The brief says who they are and what occupies them; a check flags the master approaching a
   stranger unprompted.
4. The same split for the other runners-not-servers: master of the workshops, harbourmaster,
   clerk of the counting house, master of the games.

**Research first.** GameMastery Guide settlement rules (market as base value / purchase
limit), Mount & Blade / Daggerfall / Ultima VII vendor splits, MUD shopkeepers vs wardens.

**Export.** How a settlement's trade is staffed (stall lines, a master).

### 12. Drenn's approach: "You! You have the look of someone who can navigate…"

**Cause.** The model obeyed the pull (rules/cards.py:686): "…{giver} is standing here and has
a reason to speak of it: **they approach the player and say the first word about it — in
their own words, in this beat.**" The only detail was the open objective ("Search [the wild]
for the Power leaf"), which became "do you have any idea where such a thing might be
hidden?"; "not through carelessness" is the scheme's secret leaking. No motive, no offer (the
reward never reaches the narrator). And Bobby's background tie says Drenn **taught** him —
but `acquaint` only binds the opening companion, so Drenn greets his own student as a
stranger.

**Correction.**
1. The pull carries a hook's ingredients: who the giver is and is to the player, what they
   want done, what they offer, what they are doing now, the one thing hidden (marked).
2. The approach fits the relationship — overheard, a go-between, an opening the player can
   take, greeting-then-ask for someone who knows you — chosen by the engine, not "approach
   and speak" every time.
3. One worked hook scene, uncopyable.
4. Checks: giver asks the player for the quest's own information; giver greets a known player
   as a stranger; the secret on the page.
5. `bond.knows-you` on every person a tie names.

**Research first.** The Alexandrian on hooks, Sly Flourish's strong start, the Angry GM on
motivation; how CRPGs moved off "stranger walks up with a quest" (Disco Elysium, Pentiment vs
the Oblivion pattern).

### 13. Drenn spoke to me and I am not in conversation

**Cause.** The prose tagged both of Drenn's lines correctly (`who: c4, to: you`). But
`judgement.hailed_by` (gm/judgement.py:6696) splits the beat into sentences **first** and
looks for whole quotes inside each; both quotes cross a sentence boundary ("'You!" ends a
sentence; "some…" and "Tell me…" split the second). Run on the saved beat: **no sentence
contains a span**, so neither the tag path nor the guess ran — `hails_tagged: []`,
`hails_guessed: []` beside two correct tags. Every exclamation, trailing "…" or question
inside a speech defeats it.

**Correction.** Tagged `to=you` lines are hails directly; the guess finds spans on the whole
beat and reads the sentences around them. Grep every other sentences-then-spans caller
(introductions, faces, attribution) for the same defect. Test records this beat.

### 16. Leaving the village: no walk described, and the engine stopped me anyway

"I leave the village and stand outside it. I look around to get a bearing…"

**Cause.**
- Interpreter: `leave: outside it` + `look: a bearing…`. `interpret.ops_for`
  (gm/interpret.py:323) only turns `go` to a place in town into travel; `leave` maps to
  nothing, so the plan took the nearest listed place, **the way in** (inside the gate).
- On the way the street check (rules/ontheway.py, 8%/hop) rolled a **patrol**: "The watch
  comes down the road… **You get no further.**"
- Prose reversed the direction ("the dusty road stretches out behind you… the gates of
  Vormoor open to receive you") and turned the stop into background ("the watchmen are making
  their rounds").
- The walk: the tell was only "The way there ran through the well" → half a line of prose.
- The bearing question was not answered.
- **The clock did not move**: still 08:00 after two crossings of town.
- The watchman (c1, described two beats earlier with wet-rope hair and blue knuckle-dots) was
  re-described as "an older man with a face like cracked leather… a broadsword". Why the
  brief did not carry his face was not traced.

**Correction.**
1. `leave`/"go outside" becomes a real move (item 17.1), never the nearest in-town place.
2. The walk has material: the tell carries each place passed with a detail from its own
   writing, the time taken, what is happening there at this hour; a check that the prose
   walks the route.
3. A stop is a stop: `stopped_short` in the outcomes with no stop on the page is a finding.
4. Direction checked against the move's from/to (leaving is never narrated as arriving).
5. In-town travel costs minutes.
6. Bearings answered from the world (item 19).
7. A described person keeps their face: the brief carries it; a re-description that differs
   is flagged.

### 17. It will not let me leave the settlement

"I walk to the nearest crossroads" and "I take the path away from town" — both refused, both
narrated as movement.

**Cause.** Two scales of movement and nothing between: `travel` among the town's fixed place
list, and `journey` (rules/engine.py:6617) to another named settlement, costing days. There
is no op for the road outside the gate, the fields, a crossroads. The planner tried
`found "the crossroads" kind road` → refused ("no such kind of place as 'road'" — the kinds
list has no road, crossroads, outskirts, field) → `travel` refused. The prose then wrote "You
are now on the outskirts…" with signposts to Dustgate (north) and Grotburrow (west) while the
engine held the party **at the way in, 08:00**. The only check that fired caught the word
"gate". The signpost sentences are the brief's own "ROADS OUT" wording pasted in.

**Correction.**
1. **The missing middle scale.** Every settlement has its surroundings as places: the road
   outside the gate, the fields, the shore (a stilt village), and each road out's head ("the
   head of the road to Kragmoor"). Reachable by `travel`, the natural start of a `journey`,
   and the door to open country through the existing `venture`, reachable from a typed turn.
2. The interpreter's `leave` / go-outside acts map there.
3. Road kinds foundable: road, crossroads, milestone, ford, bridge (outside ground).
4. Roads out carry their real facts in the brief (item 19).
5. **A refused move is shown as refused** — a turn whose moves all refused and whose prose
   moves the player is a finding with a repair. Raise its priority: the page currently puts
   the player somewhere the engine does not, and every later turn resolves from where the
   engine is.
6. The brief's wording must not surface verbatim in prose.

### 18. A clock that shows time passing

"A clock unmarked but by black strips, with the hour hand on the approximate before-action
time and then the minute hand spinning vigorously while the hour hand moves to the current
time… only for 1h+ time changes."

**Specification.**
- Fires when a turn advances `scene.clock_minutes` by **≥ 60**; compared before/after each
  turn response; never on load or resume.
- A plain face, **twelve black strips**, no numerals, in the table's dark-and-gold idiom.
- Hour hand from the old time; minute hand spinning while the hour hand sweeps forward
  through the whole elapsed span (never the short way back); settle, hold, fade; click to
  dismiss; never blocks input.
- Fixed ~2–3 s for any span: the minute hand spins proportionally faster, the hour hand
  makes whole turns past 12 h.
- ≥ 24 h: a words-only caption beneath ("two days later").
- Optional: the face shades across dusk/dawn.
- Reduced motion: no spin, show the end face briefly. Phone: centred over the story.
- **Depends on** 16.5 (in-town travel costs time); rests, waits and journeys already move the
  clock.

### 19. Outside town the geography is blank

"With the current narration I would assume there was endless flat desert around me except
for far off mountains."

**Cause.** The export knows plenty; the brief carries almost none of it.
- Continent Drossakar: Geography "ash-fields, iron-rich badlands, geothermal vents"; Terrain
  "volcanic ridgelines, black-rock canyons, a few fertile crater basins"; Climate "hot, dry,
  prone to ashfall storms off the active peaks".
- Vormoor: coral-and-driftwood stilt housing, on the water.
- Its routes (`play.travel`): Dustgate 72 mi road, Grotburrow 72 mi road, Scrapden 96 mi road
  — all crossing **farmland, then mountain**; Ledgerwarren and Moldwarren 192 mi by sea.
- The brief's "ROADS OUT OF VORMOOR" (gm/prompts.py:926) gives **names only** — not miles,
  road/sea, or what the road crosses, all of which `journey.legs_from` reads. No continent,
  nation or climate reaches the brief at all.
- The world has **no directions**; "north to Dustgate, west to Grotburrow" was invented, and
  three of the five routes were never mentioned.

**Correction.**
1. A "THE LAND AROUND" block whenever the party is at the edge or outside: the continent's
   geography/terrain/climate, what the roads out cross first, the coast for a port — two or
   three sentences.
2. Roads out with their real facts in words (distance as days on foot, road or sea,
   crossings); no invented compass bearings.
3. The outside places (17.1) take their terrain from the same list, so what is described and
   where one can go agree.
4. A check: a beat outside town with nothing from that list gets a repair asking for one
   physical detail (the opening's drawn-from-the-place check, the shape that held).
5. A compass bearing attached to a destination the world gave none is cut or repaired.

**Export.** Relative position/direction between settlements (or coordinates); the terrain
immediately around each settlement, its coast, and where each road leaves it.

### 20. Into the "gnarled trees" — and still in Vormoor

"I head west into the gnarled dense trees" → panel VORMOOR · VILLAGE · FOREST · 9H IN.

**Cause.**
- The move did happen: `travel biome=forest` → `bde94b038cba~forest:the-approach`, clock
  08:00 → 09:00. But the ground outside is filed under the village's own id, so the panel,
  the place list and every next move still read as Vormoor — only `journey` changes the
  settlement. That is the "pulling me back".
- The forest was **the model's**: the previous beat invented "trees grow denser to the west"
  while the engine held the party at the gate; the engine accepted `biome: forest` unchecked.
  Drossakar is ash-fields, badlands, volcanic ridges and crater basins; "crushed pine and damp
  earth" is invented too.
- Map caption for "the approach" reads "the way in, and little to hide behind" — the gate's.
- The man with the skinning knife speaks and asks a question but was not in the scene (only
  the PC); he became c8 a turn later only because the player addressed him.
- "The road to Grotburrow isn't for the faint of heart" — no journey was begun.

**Correction.**
1. Outside a settlement reads as outside ("near Vormoor · forest", or the region's name) in
   the panel, the brief and the place list — the middle scale of 17.1.
2. Biome moves are checked against the world's geography; an absent terrain is redirected in
   words ("no woodland here; the nearest cover is the black-rock canyon").
3. Every place has its own caption.
4. A speaker the prose introduces is made real through the one entrance (with a square) or
   cut.
5. A named road is claimed only when a journey or a road-head place puts the party on it.

### 21. "I cast burning hands into the tree tops" did nothing; the Spells tab only aims at people

**Cause.**
- The refusal was **correct**: Bobby had **nothing prepared** (`prepared: {}` in the save
  before that turn; Burning Hands appears only in the next save, after the player prepared it
  in the Spells tab).
- But the player never learned it: seven plan attempts refused with the same
  "did not prepare Burning Hands today", a hand-off to the second model, then `narrate_only`;
  and the false-claim check misread "into the tree tops" as the player claiming to *produce*
  tree tops, so the man mocked a bluff ("so little fire").
- A prepared caster starts a campaign with a spellbook of 50 and nothing prepared; no prompt,
  no default, no warning.
- The Spells tab can aim only at a creature (or self, or nothing); the `cast` op has no aim at
  a point or an object.

**Correction.**
1. **The cast button attaches, it does not send**: the spell lands in the input as a chip;
   the player writes what they do with it; if they write nothing, the model infers the likely
   use from the scene. The chip carries the spell id. One route for typed and attached casts;
   the footer button of item 11 behaves the same.
2. Aim at points and things: `cast` takes a non-creature aim (direction, point, object);
   areas record what they caught; fire in brush or a canopy has engine-owned consequences
   (burning terrain, smoke, spread) through the hazard rules, never the narrator.
3. **A refusal the player can fix goes to the player at once** — unprepared, no slots, not in
   the book, not your turn: stop after the first, show it plainly with a button to prepare;
