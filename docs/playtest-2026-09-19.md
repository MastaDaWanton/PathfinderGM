# Play-test, 2026-09-19 — eight items, investigated

Reported by the player on the 0.1.9 build. No code changed while this was written: each
item was traced to the line that causes it, the prior art was searched before any design
was drawn, and the plan is at the end.

Three of the eight turn out to be one missing idea (a group of people as one thing), and
one of them is a regression this project introduced yesterday. Two are smaller than they
look; one is much larger.

---

## 1. Class kits should become starting gold

**Reported.** "Remove the starting items from classes and give them extra starting gold,
we have the outfitter now."

**Found.** The kits are eleven hand-written rows, `rules/creation.py:113-131`, read
exactly once when a sheet is assembled (`rules/creation.py:576-609`). Nothing else in the
app grants starting gear — `rules/leveling.py` has no kit writer, class documents carry
no equipment field, and a homebrew class already gets nothing but `unarmed`.

The surprise: **starting gold is already rolled.** `creation.starting_purse`
(`rules/creation.py:201-232`) parses the class's `starting_wealth` dice — `5d6 x 10 gp`
for a fighter, `2d6 x 10` for a wizard — and rolls it into `Actor.purse`. So a character
today gets the Core Rulebook's full starting wealth *and* a free kit on top. Two shipped
documents say otherwise and are simply wrong (`rules/classbuilder.py:417`,
`docs/class-creation.md:50`), and a test pins the falsehood
(`tests/test_classbuilder.py:114`).

The outfitter (`play/outfit_views.py`, 192 lines) runs *after* the kit is granted: the
forge POSTs with `begin: false` and redirects to `/outfit/`. It is re-enterable but
linked from nowhere except that redirect. Its catalogue is every priced weapon, armour,
shield and every entry in `goods.GEAR`, and the purse is a hard wall.

**A blocker, measured.** Three kit weapons cannot be bought back:

| item | price in the app | Core Rulebook |
|---|---|---|
| quarterstaff | `cost_gp: null` | — (free) |
| club | `cost_gp: null` | — (free) |
| light crossbow | absent from `content/weapons/weapons.json` | 35 gp |

The outfitter skips anything whose cost is `None`, `""` or `0` (`outfit_views.py:42`), so
those three are silently missing from its shelves — and the cleric, druid, monk, sorcerer
and wizard kits are built out of exactly them. Remove the kits today and a monk cannot
buy a quarterstaff.

**Prior art.** The Core Rulebook is unambiguous and the app is the one that invented
kits: ["each character begins play with an outfit worth 10 gp or
less"](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/equipment/) and nothing else beyond the
[starting wealth table](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/character-creation/)
(fighter 5d6×10, average 175 gp; cleric 4d6×10, 140; wizard 2d6×10, 70). That wealth is
calibrated to buy the kit: a fighter's kit is chain shirt 100 + longsword 15 + dagger 2 +
light crossbow 35 + heavy shield 20 = **172 gp** against an average of **175**.

**Plan.** Delete `KITS` and grant nothing but the free outfit, which `OUTFITS`
(`creation.py:94-102`) already does and which is the rulebook's own exception. The purse
is untouched — it is already right — and the "extra gold" the player asked for is the
gold that was always there and had been quietly pre-spent. Before that lands: price the
free weapons at 0 and let the outfitter stock a 0-gp item (a quarterstaff is free in the
book, not unbuyable), and import the light crossbow. Keep the two objects that are class
*features* rather than equipment — the wizard's spellbook, which the rulebook grants
free, and whatever a divine caster needs as a focus — or a cleric cannot cast. Link the
outfitter from the roster so it can be re-entered on purpose. Fix the two stale documents
and the test that pins them; `tests/test_outfit.py:48` asserts a fighter's longsword and
will need to buy one first.

## 2. Spellcasting

**Reported.** "Spellcasting is broken in general. A lvl 1 cleric can cast wall of flame
at 6th level, this is obviously an error." Plus the rules the player wants: clerics know
the whole list and prepare into slots by level; wizards learn into a book and prepare from
it; and a screen at character creation for choosing spells and filling slots.

**Found — and it is not the error it looks like.** The engine's legality gate is complete
and correct. `_check_cast` (`rules/engine.py:6555-6627`) makes nine checks in order,
including *"level 5 spell, level 1 cleric"*, and it was exercised live during this
investigation on a real level-1 cleric:

```
flame-strike        → cast: Flame Strike is a level 5 spell and Ted is a level 1 cleric
                      — they reach level 1.
cure-light-wounds   → resolved. "Ted casts Cure Light Wounds (caster level 1 … DC 15). 1d8+1 — 9."
```

Nothing was cast. The spell never became an intent at all, and the beat the player read
was pure narration. The gap is a chain of three:

1. **Clerics never prepare anything.** `CASTERS["cleric"]` carries
   `prepare_from: "list"` (`rules/casting.py:149`), and `casting.knows` reads that as
   "knows everything, prepares nothing" (`rules/casting.py:313-315`). The prepared check
   in `_check_cast` is conditional on `prepare_from == "spellbook"`
   (`rules/engine.py:6617`). So a cleric's `spellbook` and `prepared` stay **empty for
   the life of the character**. Druids, paladins and rangers are the same.
2. **An empty book means the declaration never reaches the engine.**
   `judgement.inject_cast` builds its vocabulary from exactly those two fields
   (`gm/judgement.py:3403-3404`) and bows out when they are empty (`:3416-3417`). So
   `declared_ops` never reports `cast`, the turn schema never requires one, and out of
   combat there is no op restriction at all — `narrate_only` is legal and the model takes
   it.
3. **The model is told nothing.** The brief (`gm/prompts.py:860-945`) carries hit points,
   states, carried jars, purse, race and background — and no spell list, no prepared
   spells, no slot counts, no highest castable level. The word "spell" appears six times
   in the whole prompt file and never as a capability.

So the model had no true list to cite and nothing stopping it inventing one. It did:
`wall-of-fire.lists` is `{wizard: 4, sorcerer: 4, druid: 5, …}` with **no cleric entry at
all** — the spell is not on the cleric list in the first place.

**Evidence, from the player's own screen.** A Cleric 1 typed *"I stand in the street and
put my hand out in a 'halt' position and cast wall of flame at 6th level"*, and the beat
delivered the wall of fire in full — "the magic of your casting roaring to life … a
towering curtain of flame". The character panel beside it, after the beat:

| | |
|---|---|
| Spell Slot 0 | **3 of 3** |
| Spell Slot 1 | **4 of 4** |

Not one slot spent. That is the whole diagnosis in two rows: the engine was never asked,
so it never refused. Had the declaration reached it, `_check_cast` would have answered
that Wall of Fire is not on the cleric list at all.

Two smaller truths found on the way. The slot machinery is real and right: full tables in
`rules/casting.py:28-133`, slots are ordinary resource pools that refresh on a night's
rest, and a live WIS 18 level-1 cleric has `{0: 3, 1: 2}`. And the preparation endpoint
already enforces the rule the player asked for — `play/views.py:903-909` counts what is
prepared at a level against the slots at that level, with the comment "a wizard cannot
memorise five fireballs into two slots". What is missing is a screen that can browse a
class list to prepare *from*: the Spells tab (`table.html:3670-3717`) only offers a `+`
on rows already in the book, so a cleric's page reads "Nothing in the book yet." forever.

**Evidence — the Spells tab of that same Cleric 1.** Every number on the page is right,
and the page has no door:

| Slots | | The rest |
|---|---|---|
| Level 0 | 3 of 3 | Caster level 1 |
| Level 1, DC 21 | 4 of 4 | Casting ability WIS, highest spell level 1 |

Three orisons and four first-level slots is exactly correct for a Cleric 1 with WIS 30
(one base plus three bonus), and DC 21 is exactly 10 + 1 + 10. Beneath the slots the page
prints the class note — *"A cleric prepares from the whole cleric list — their god is the
book"* — and beside it, under **Prepared today**, it prints *"Nothing in the book yet."*
One screen says the list is the book; the other says the book is empty; and there is no
control anywhere to put a spell in either. The message itself is a wizard's, shown to a
character the code has decided does not own a book.
Level-up grants no spells to anyone (`rules/leveling.py` has no spell writer), and the
forge's only spell input is a free-text list of spell ids shown to wizards, sorcerers and
bards; a cleric is actively refused ("A cleric picks no spells at creation",
`rules/creation.py:541`).

**Prior art.** The player's rules are the Core Rulebook's, quoted from
[Chapter 9: Magic](https://www.aonprd.com/Rules.aspx?Name=Magic&Category=Chapter%209:%20Magic)
and the [Wizard](https://www.aonprd.com/ClassDisplay.aspx?ItemName=Wizard) class:

- Clerics prepare "through prayer and meditation at a particular time of day"; the number
  per day comes from class level and Wisdom.
- "A good cleric (or a cleric of a good deity) can spontaneously cast a cure spell in
  place of a prepared spell of the same level or higher, but not in place of a bonus
  domain spell." Evil clerics substitute inflict spells.
- A wizard "begins with a spellbook containing all 0-level wizard spells … plus three
  1st-level spells of his choice", plus one more per point of Intelligence modifier, and
  "at each new wizard level, he gains two new spells".
- "He cannot prepare any spell not recorded in his spellbook."
- Preparation follows 8 hours' rest plus an hour with the book.
- One refinement on the player's version: the book lets a caster "fill a higher-level
  spell slot with a lower-level spell", never the reverse. That satisfies both of the
  player's prohibitions and is strictly more generous, so it is what should ship.

For the screen itself, the two Pathfinder CRPGs settle the shape: learning and preparing
are [two separate steps on the same book](https://pathfinderkingmaker.fandom.com/wiki/Spells)
— you choose what you know at level-up, and choose what is in your head after a rest —
and the commonest complaint about both games is the [preparation UI
itself](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1184370/discussions/0/2963922521560709524), which
is a warning about the screen being a list of three thousand things.

**Plan.** In four parts, and the first is the one that fixes the reported bug.

1. **Make `prepare_from: "list"` mean what it says.** A cleric, druid, paladin or ranger
   prepares from the whole class list instead of being treated as knowing it. One change
   in `casting.knows`, one in `_check_cast`'s prepared condition. The moment a cleric's
   `prepared` is non-empty, `inject_cast` has a vocabulary, the declaration becomes a
   `cast` intent, and the gate that already works starts working for them.
2. **A screen to prepare from, in both places it is needed.** The endpoint exists and
   already refuses correctly; what is needed is a browser, and the same component serves
   the forge (wizards choose what goes in the book; every prepared caster then fills
   slots) and the rest in play. Wizards also need the rulebook's starting book (all
   0-level + 3 + Int modifier) and two new spells per level in `rules/leveling.py`.

   **The panel gets a third region: what this character knows** (asked for 2026-09-19).
   Today it shows *Slots* and *Prepared today* and nothing in between, which is why the
   page has no door. Known means a different thing per caster and the app already records
   which — so one function, `casting.known_spells(actor)`, answers it for all of them:

   | caster | `prepare_from` | known is |
   |---|---|---|
   | wizard | `spellbook` | the book they carry |
   | sorcerer, bard | `known` | their repertoire |
   | cleric, druid, paladin, ranger | `list` | the whole class list — their god is the book |

   Rendered grouped by spell level, with the levels they can cast open and the rest shown
   but closed as *not yet* — so "all known" is answered honestly without putting the whole
   thing on one page. Measured against the shipped corpus, that grouping is not optional:

   | | spells |
   |---|---|
   | the cleric list, whole | **1,143** |
   | the cleric list a level 1 cleric can cast (levels 0 and 1) | **179** |
   | orisons alone | 20 |
   | the pool a 1st-level wizard's starting book is chosen from (levels 0 and 1) | 280 |

   So a Cleric 1 opens on 20 orisons and 159 first-level spells with everything above
   folded away, and each row at a castable level carries the button that fills a slot —
   the door the page is missing. At that size it needs search and a level filter; a
   wizard's book is a dozen rows and needs neither, though the *choosing* screen a wizard
   meets at creation and level-up browses the same 280 and does.

   **And that same function should feed the declaration path.** `inject_cast` currently
   builds its vocabulary from the book and the prepared list, which for a cleric is
   empty. Pointed at `known_spells` it recognises "I cast wall of flame" from any caster,
   turns it into a `cast` intent, and lets the engine answer properly — *"Wall of Fire is
   not on the cleric list"*, or *"you have not prepared it today"* — instead of the
   declaration drifting into prose because nothing recognised it. A refusal the player
   can read is the whole point; silence is what produced the bug.
3. **Spontaneous cure conversion**, per the quoted rule: a cleric casting a cure spell
   may sacrifice any prepared spell of that level or higher; the engine picks the lowest
   that serves and the tell names what was given up. Alignment decides cure or inflict.
4. **Close the narration hole, because none of the above stops prose.** The brief lists
   prepared spells and slots remaining, by level, so the model has something true to
   cite. And a new mechanical check, the same shape as `wrong-hands` and
   `swing-not-yet-struck`: a beat that names a spell the engine did not cast this turn is
   a finding, rewritten once and cut as the backstop. Nothing in `gm/narration.py` or
   `rules/intents.py` looks for a spell name today.

This is the largest item on the list and should be its own release.

## 2b. Four core classes grant nothing at any level

**Reported.** The Class tab of that same cleric: *The whole table*, levels 1 to 20, and
the "What it grants" column reads **—** on every one of the twenty rows.

**Found.** The table is honest; the document behind it is empty. Classes live in two
places, and only one of them carries a progression:

| where | classes | level table |
|---|---|---|
| `content/classes/*.json` | barbarian, bard, blood-bending, druid, monk, paladin, ranger, sorcerer | **20 rows of `grants`** — the druid's first row is `["nature bond", "nature sense", "orisons", "wild empathy"]` |
| `rules/tables.py CLASSES` | **cleric, fighter, wizard, rogue** | **none** — the keys are `bab`, `good_saves`, `hit_die`, `class_skills`, `proficiencies`, `skill_ranks`, `starting_wealth` and nothing else |

So four of the classes a player is most likely to pick are a chassis with no features.
The cleric has no channel energy, no domains, no spontaneous casting; the fighter no
bonus feats, no bravery, no weapon training; the wizard no arcane school or bond; and the
rogue no sneak attack — the phrase appears exactly once in the whole app, in
`rules/glossary.py`, as a definition nothing reads.

What the rulebook puts in the cleric's rows alone, from the
[class entry](https://www.aonprd.com/ClassDisplay.aspx?ItemName=Cleric): aura, orisons,
channel energy and two domains at 1st level, with channel energy "equal to 1d6 points of
damage plus 1d6 points of damage for every two cleric levels beyond 1st (2d6 at 3rd, 3d6
at 5th, and so on)", and — the part that touches item 2 directly — "a cleric gains one
domain spell slot for each level of cleric spell she can cast, from 1st on up", plus the
domain powers themselves.

**Why it belongs beside item 2.** The domain slot *is* a spell slot, so the slots panel
is wrong by one row per level until domains exist, and the spontaneous conversion rule is
worded as an exception to it ("but not in place of a bonus domain spell"). Building the
cleric's preparation without domains means building the slot arithmetic twice.

### Domains, chosen at creation (asked for 2026-09-19)

**What the app already holds.** The spell corpus knows the domains: **153 distinct domain
names** across 452 spells, carried as strings like `"Luck (2), Tactics (2)"` — the domain
and the level at which that domain grants that spell. So every domain's **spell list is
derivable from the corpus today**, with no new data.

What is *not* there is the other half of a domain: its **powers** (the Fire domain's fire
bolt, the Travel domain's agile feet). Those are in no shipped file and would have to be
authored, as effect documents, the same way feats and class abilities already are.

**Whose gods? — RULING, 2026-09-19.** "Grab whatever the belief system of the world is
and let that be enough. Don't worry about the gods or the alignment."

The world had already written that answer itself. Its own `Metaphysics` fact reads:

> **"Gods are real, distant, and plural; no single faith holds the whole world."**

So there is no pantheon to list and nothing to gate on. No deity step, no alignment
restriction, no god named anywhere — and the Core Rulebook's own wording for a cleric not
devoted to a particular deity covers it exactly: she "still selects two domains to
represent her spiritual inclinations and abilities". That is the whole of the choice.

**Her faith is read, not chosen.** Every piece of it is already in the export and already
reachable from the sheet — `world_people_id` is stamped at creation
(`rules/creation.py:583`) and two existing callers already resolve it to the people
entity (`play/campaign.py:588`, `play/opening_prose.py:244`):

| from | fact | Aurvantis, for a human cleric |
|---|---|---|
| the world | Metaphysics | "Gods are real, distant, and plural; no single faith holds the whole world" |
| the world | Cosmology | "A single sun, twin moons (Aurel and Vantis), one continuous ocean, four continents" |
| her people | Belief | "Belief varies wildly by nation, often eclectic" |
| her people | Rites | "Rites of passage tied to the local calendar and the twin-moon cycle" |
| her people | Taboos | "Oath-breaking and hospitality violations are near-universally condemned" |

That is what the sheet should show under her class, and what the brief should state as
fact so the narrator writes her devotions correctly — she swears by the twin moons
because her people do, not because a model invented a god. A world that ships named gods
later can supply a list the forge offers instead; this one does not, and needs none.

So: **the cleric picks two domains at creation from the 153 the corpus knows, and nothing
else.** No deity, no alignment gate, no second screen.

**The domain slot, quoted so it is built once and correctly:**

> "A cleric gains one domain spell slot for each level of cleric spell she can cast, from
> 1st on up. Each day, a cleric can prepare one of the spells from her two domains in
> that slot. If a domain spell is not on the cleric spell list, a cleric can prepare it
> only in her domain spell slot. Domain spells cannot be used to cast spells
> spontaneously."

Three consequences for the panel and the engine: the slots list gains a **domain row per
castable level** beside the ordinary ones; a domain spell that is not on the cleric list
is preparable *only* there; and the spontaneous cure conversion of item 2 must refuse to
sacrifice a domain slot.

**Plan for domains.** A `rules/domains.py` built by reading the corpus for the spell
lists, plus authored powers per domain as ordinary effect documents. One field on the
sheet, the chosen pair. A forge step for the cleric that picks two from the 153, on the
same screen that picks her prepared spells and one step ahead of it — no gate, no deity,
nothing to approve. `casting.slots_for` learns the domain row; `known_spells` includes
both domains' lists; preparation routes a domain spell to the domain slot; spontaneous
conversion refuses it. The faith itself is read from the world and shown, never asked
for. The powers can land after the slots do: a cleric with domains that grant spells and
no powers yet is playable, a cleric with no domains at all is not.

**Plan.** Move the four stubs into `content/classes/` with real 20-row tables, the same
shape the other eight already use and the class builder already writes. Do the cleric
with item 2, because its domain slot and its channel energy are part of that arithmetic.
The other three are a separate piece of work of their own size — the rogue's sneak attack
in particular is a damage rule the engine will have to apply, not a line of prose — and
each should be checked against its own rulebook page the way the cleric's was here rather
than written from memory.

## 3. A map at all times, and spawns that fit the situation

**Reported.** "A map should be displayed at all times and Enemy/NPC spawns should not be
random but based on the situation and narration should match it."

**Found, the map.** The grid exists only inside a fight, by construction. It is laid in
one helper (`rules/engine.py:7060`, `_lay_battlefield`) called from two places, both of
them the start of a fight; it is set to `None` by `end_encounter`
(`rules/engine.py:1118`); and it is gated again server-side (`play/views.py:52`) and in
the browser, which prints "No ground is mapped. A grid is laid out when a fight starts"
(`table.html:2261-2270`).

Nothing is missing to derive one. `floorplan.for_place(place_id, terrain, authored)` is a
pure function — the scatter is a SHA-seeded LCG, not `random` — and all three inputs
(`scene.at`, `places.terrain_of`, `here().shape`) are available on every turn. There is
even a `floorplan.describe()` that renders the ground in prose and is called by nothing
in the repo. What is genuinely absent is **positions**: `end_encounter` clears them and
every placer returns early when there is no grid. The zone word (`engaged` / `near` /
`far`) survives on every actor at all times, which is exactly the input
`place_by_zone` needs.

**Found, the spawns.** There is no randomness anywhere in the spawn path — no `random`
import in any of it, `_free_spot_at` and `npcs.choose` are both explicitly deterministic.
The real complaint is appropriateness, and it is well founded: who appears is decided by
**three regexes over the player's own sentence** (`gm/judgement.py:371-381`) falling
through to a literal `"thug"` or `"guildhand"`, and the model is offered exactly four
templates (`rules/engine.py:1411`) although 7,133 stat blocks are loaded and reachable.

The situational material is already written and unread:

- `places.STAFFED` — 25 rows, each a place with who is there and the words to search the
  bestiary with (`"the guardhouse": ("the watch", "the sergeant of the watch", ("guard",
  "watch", "sergeant"))`). Its only caller in the repo is a tooling script.
- `places.category_of` — seven kinds of place; read only to decide whether a keeper has a
  counter.
- `npcs.choose` with a CR band around the party (`target_cr(level) = level - 1`).
- The world export: a `tension` sentence per settlement, 96 cards with people and places,
  16 conflicts. The brief tells the model none of it — no terrain, no staffing, no
  tension.

One measured limit on ambition: in the shipped world all 739 places carry
`terrain: "urban"` and all 256 cast entries carry the role `"Person"`, so terrain and the
cast's roles cannot discriminate. The place *slug* (`the-well`, `the-market`) is the
usable key — which is precisely what `STAFFED` and `floorplan.BY_SPOT` are already keyed
on.

**Prior art.** Virtual tabletops show the map always and treat the tactical layer as a
toggle on top of it: Foundry's [Tactical Map](https://foundryvtt.com/packages/tactical-map)
module exists to let a theatre-of-the-mind game "solve combats more tactically", and
turning off token vision shows the whole playfield. For spawns, the reference design is
Left 4 Dead's Director, which places enemies "based upon each player's current situation,
status, skill, and location" with ["structured
unpredictability"](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=147309463) and
a set of **threat locations the map author placed**. This app has both halves already:
the places are the threat locations, and `scene.heat` plus the cards are the intensity.

**Plan.**

- **Map.** Lay the floorplan when the party arrives somewhere, not when a fight starts;
  keep it through `end_encounter` (clear it on travel instead); place everyone by their
  zone word so there are positions out of combat; drop the two `grid is None` early
  returns. Render it quietly out of a fight — no initiative, no sides, the same ground —
  and let the fight add the tactical layer, which is what the VTTs do. `describe()` gives
  the map a caption for free.
- **Spawns.** One chooser, `who_would_be_here(scene, world)`, reading in order: the
  place's `STAFFED` words, the live cards' people, the settlement's own residents, then
  the place category, and only then a default — resolved against the bestiary by
  `npcs.choose` inside the CR band. The four-template hint becomes "the people this place
  holds", so the model picks from a real roster instead of inventing one. The count and
  the kind must match the prose that introduced them, which is item 5.

## 4. "I turn to find the mayor" spawned a mayor

**Reported.** "When I say that I turn to find the mayor, a character who was not in the
scene, the Narrator spawned them instead of saying I couldn't find them."

**Found.** Six repairs in `gm/judgement.py` can emit a `spawn` because the player named
somebody. The culprit here is `repair_unknown_refs` (`:869`), and the mechanism was
reproduced: the model writes a target `"mayor"`, validation raises a `refs` error,
`_INVENTED_REF` matches the word, and because exactly one ref was invented **the ref
becomes the actor's name**. No template cue matches "mayor", so the default fires. A
13-hp Warrior-1 called *mayor*, carrying a sap, walks into the scene.

**Not one of the six consults the world.** None of them takes a `world` argument. And the
world can answer both halves of the question:

- `World.by_name` / `World.all_named` — is there anybody of this name or office at all?
  Their only caller is the `/gm` lookup tool, never the turn path.
- `World.residents(settlement)` — who lives here. One caller, also `/gm`.
- `Engine._elsewhere(ref)` — already prints *"c4 (Drenn Ironvale) is at the market, not
  here. Leave them out of it this turn."* — but it keys on a **ref**, so a person who was
  never instantiated has no ref and falls through to the branch that invents them.

The engine already refuses absent people correctly everywhere money or bodies are
involved: "There is no {buyer} here to sell to", "There is no body here to loot", "There
is no {target} here to use it on". A *sale* to an absent person is dropped and the turn
survives (`drop_unfulfillable_trades`); an *attack* or a *talk* naming one spawns them.
That asymmetry is the whole bug.

**Evidence.** Seen again on the same screen as item 2, one beat later: *"I turn to the
mayor of the town and ask him 'What kind of reward do you plan to give to the savior of
the town?'"* → *"The mayor stands before you, his heavy woolen cloak still smelling of
woodsmoke…"*. He was not there a moment earlier, and the town has no mayor.

And the world's own answer to this particular question is good. Among the 256 characters
there is no mayor anywhere — the roles are `law-speaker` (19), `fleet pilot` (18),
`harbor-reeve` (13), `guildmaster` (10) and so on — while every city's `Formal Power`
fact names an *office* and never a person ("A local reeve confirmed by Ashgate Reach's
central authority"). Cross-matching all 256 names against all 1,024 city facts returns
zero hits.

**Prior art.** Parser interactive fiction solved this with *scope*: "You can't see any
such thing" is Inform's answer for a thing out of scope, and the tradition distinguishes
the cases — [some games answer differently](https://intfiction.org/t/i7-npc-scope-and-the-can-see-check/47889)
for an object that exists but is elsewhere ("You can't see that now") versus a word the
game does not know ("I don't know the word"). Three states, three answers.

**Plan.** Ask the world before anybody is created, and give the three answers:

1. **Here** — nothing to do.
2. **Real, but elsewhere** — "the harbour-reeve keeps the quay; he is not here",
   printed by code from the world's own record, with the place named so the player can
   go there. This is `_elsewhere`'s sentence, reached by name instead of by ref.
3. **No such person** — "there is no mayor in Vormoor; the town keeps to a reeve",
   built from the settlement's own `Formal Power` fact. Never a spawn.

The narrow exception stays: `repair_unknown_refs` exists because the *prose* described
people arriving and the model then could not name them, and that is legitimate. The test
becomes whose word it was — the player naming somebody is a question, the narration
introducing somebody is an arrival.

## 5. Raiders in the prose and nobody on the board

**Reported.** "The narration continues to add actors in prose without spawning them in. A
group of raiders entering the town should not be narration only but should have as many
raiders spawned as is described … also they were initially described as soldiers and then
became raiders."

**Found, and worse than reported.** `raider` is in no form anywhere in `_CAST_ROLES`
(`gm/judgement.py:3753-3767`). Measured:

```
'a band of twelve raiders'   → note_cast books []   promote_cast makes []
'a band of twelve soldiers'  → books one entry: {'who': 'twelve soldier', 'count': 4}
```

So raiders are invisible: zero ledger entries, zero actors, not even a mention in
`cast_brief`. And the soldiers case is wrong three ways at once. `split_collective_name`
returns 4 for any uncounted collective; `_NUMBER_WORDS` has no entry for **twelve**, so
the number is neither read nor stripped and the ledger books a person literally called
*"twelve soldier"*; and the count is clamped to `_PROMOTED_CAP = 4` at
`judgement.py:3959` before promotion is even reached. Twelve in the prose, four on the
board — three if one bystander is already standing, and **zero if a fight is running**,
because `promote_cast` returns immediately mid-encounter.

The template would be wrong too: "raider" matches none of the three cues, so a raider
becomes a 4-hp guildhand with a club. The irony is that the bestiary already ships a
`raider` — CR 2, 29 hp, speed 40, worth 600 XP — and `spawn {"template": "raider"}` would
work today. Only the cue table cannot reach it. Twenty-two soldier blocks are in there too,
and the cue sends "soldier" to the 11-hp watchman instead.

**Evidence.** The same screen again, two beats earlier: *"a line of figures silhouetted
against the gray morning light, their forms heavy and armored"*, and by the end of the
next beat *"the **soldiers** ahead of you are forced to halt"*. Soldiers is the word this
scene opened with; raiders is what they had become by the time the player reported it.
The word "soldier" is bookable and would have put watchmen on the board — but the figures
arrived as "a line of figures", which matches nothing, so the board stayed empty and
there was never a booked word for the next beat to contradict.

**The drift.** The only thing holding a role word across beats is one sentence of prompt
text — `cast_brief`'s "keep them consistent, do not re-introduce them". Nothing compares
the next beat's role nouns against the ledger; nothing rejects a beat that renames
soldiers to raiders. On the reported turn the first word was bookable and the second was
not, so the two were never reconciled by anything at all.

**Plan.** Three fixes and a fourth that item 8 supplies.

- Widen `_CAST_ROLES` with the words prose actually uses for armed strangers — raider,
  brigand, bandit, reaver, mercenary, sellsword, brute, ruffian — and route them to the
  bestiary blocks that already exist rather than to the four hand-written templates. The
  cue table becomes a lookup against the corpus, which is item 3's chooser.
- Read the whole number: `_NUMBER_WORDS` gains the teens and the tens, and the count is
  taken from the prose rather than defaulted to four. A phrase that states a number is
  not a guess.
- Let prose promote people **during** a fight. A band arriving mid-battle is exactly when
  it matters; the current early return is why they stay prose.
- Hold the name mechanically: a booked entry's role word is the name, and a later beat
  calling them something else is repaired to the booked word — the same shape as the
  un-namer, which already replaces a name the scene does not hold.

The cap is the real tension: four bodies is right for a crowd of extras and wrong for
twelve raiders. Item 8 resolves it — twelve raiders are one unit, not twelve actors.

## 6. The stranger refuses to give his name

**Reported.** "Stranger on the stairs refuses to give his name."

**Evidence.** On the same screen as items 2, 4 and 5: *"the stranger beside you stands,
their face illuminated by the flames"*, and *"the stranger says"*. Still the stranger,
still nameless, several scenes in.

**Found — this is a regression, introduced yesterday.** The name machinery built on
2026-09-18 draws a true name for every promoted person from the world's own pools and
renames the panel when the name is given in play. It worked in the replays. Then, on the
second replay, the narrator began *using* those names before anybody had asked — "Soren's
eyes narrow", "the woman beside you, Kael Throk" — so the name was removed from the brief
entirely (`gm/prompts.py:957-964`, and `docs/names-and-faces.md` records the reasoning).

The consequence was not thought through: the model now has no name to give, and
`settle_introductions` only *replaces* a name the model has already offered. Asked
outright, the model does the only thing left to it and refuses.

**Plan.** Two halves, neither of which puts the name back in the brief.

- **On the turn the player asks, give it.** `judgement._ASKS_A_NAME` already detects the
  question — it was written yesterday for the bare-quoted-answer case. On that turn only,
  the name goes into the prompt as the thing he says, and the existing
  `apply_introductions` renames the panel. If the beat still refuses, the engine's own
  line stands in.
- **Make "does he tell you" a fact, not a whim.** `rules/attitude.py` already holds the
  Core Rulebook's attitude track and its influence DCs. Indifferent or better gives a
  name; unfriendly or hostile refuses, and a Diplomacy check can move them. Then a
  refusal is legitimate, legible and actionable — which is what the player asked for
  originally ("if he refuses to give one that is fine") — instead of the only thing the
  model can do.

## 7. Drenn Ironvale and the merchant, undescribed

**Reported.** "Drenn Ironvale and the merchant are in scene without having been
described."

**Found.** Four writers put people into a scene, and the description check covers none of
them:

| writer | who |
|---|---|
| `campaign.new_campaign:474-477` | the opening companion |
| `keepers.staff` | the one person behind a counter — **this is the Drenn Ironvale path** |
| `schemes._role_for` | a world resident named by a scheme |
| `engine._bring_in` | anything the `spawn` op creates |

`narration.faceless` is called in exactly one loop, over the phrases `note_cast` booked
from *this turn's* prose; `judgement.examined` covers the one person the player's own
sentence looked over. Everyone else — every keeper, every scheme cast member, everyone
promoted on an earlier turn, everyone loaded from a save — can be referenced by the
narrator for the rest of the campaign without one sentence describing them. There is no
`described` flag on `Actor` at all.

Keepers are worse off still. `keepers.name_for` composes a world-*shaped* name out of the
local name stock, so "Drenn Ironvale" may be no world character at all, and their
`world_entity_id` is a synthetic `keeper:<place>`. `names.resident_appearance` looks that
up, finds nothing, and returns `""` — so a keeper has no appearance, their brief line has
no `Looks` clause, and there is nothing for a check to enforce even if one ran.

All 256 world characters do carry an Appearance fact ("carries an old scar earned young
and never explained"), and it does reach the brief for anyone whose `world_entity_id` is
real.

**Plan.** One bit and one rule. Put `described: bool` on the actor, set when a beat
carries appearance words about them (`faceless` already knows how to ask). The first beat
in which an undescribed person acts, speaks or is addressed must describe them, or the
engine appends their own line — the resident's Appearance fact, or the people's body
line. Give keepers a real appearance at the moment they are staffed, from the settlement's
people, since their synthetic id will never resolve. This is the same machinery as the
faceless check, moved from "booked this turn" to "not described yet", which is the
condition that was actually meant.

## 8. A crowd as one thing

**Reported.** "A crowd of people should be spawned as a single unit with the combined hp
of all its members, it should take up however many squares the people would take up, find
a way to display this and allow for them to be killed giving xp, run away and scatter."

**Found.** The only collective code in the app does the opposite on purpose.
`bestiary.split_collective_name` turns "a pair of guards" into two actors, and its
docstring records why: a single 11-hp actor called "pair of guards" meant "the player
fought half as many people as the fiction described". So this is a design reversal, not a
gap — and the earlier decision was right for two guards and wrong for a crowd.

Three things make it much cheaper than it sounds:

1. **The grid already honours multi-square creatures.** `grid.footprint` and
   `tables.SPACE_AND_REACH` give 1 square for medium, 4 for large, 9 for huge, 16 for
   gargantuan, 36 for colossal, and every geometric reader — distance, reach, cover,
   flanking, reachability — respects it.
2. **The map already draws them.** `table.html:2330-2351` renders an `n × n` rectangle
   from `a.squares`, which `play/views.py:206` already serves. A crowd unit would appear
   as a 3×3 block today with no template change.
3. **The corpus already ships them.** 22 stat blocks carry the `troop` subtype and 41
   carry `swarm` — a goblin troop at 52 hp, an imperial infantry troop at 126 — but
   `subtype` is stripped before the Actor is built (`bestiary.py:288`), so the engine has
   never known that a troop is a troop.

What genuinely needs building is the rule set, the attrition, the morale and the readout.
`xp.worth` pays one all-or-nothing award per fallen actor, and a fight the enemy flees
pays nothing at all — deliberately, so that mercy is not taxed.

**Prior art**, and it is unusually good:

- **Pathfinder's own troop subtype**
  ([Archives of Nethys](https://www.aonprd.com/MonsterSubtypes.aspx?ItemName=Troop)) is
  the mechanic the player described. "A single troop occupies a 20-foot-by-20-foot
  square"; it makes no attack rolls but "deals automatic damage to any creature within
  reach or whose space they occupy at the end of their move"; it "attempts saving throws
  as a single creature"; it is "immune to any spell or effect that targets a specific
  number of creatures" and takes +50% from area effects; and "reducing a troop to 0 hit
  points or fewer causes it to break up, effectively destroying the troop". Rendered
  unconscious by nonlethal damage, "it disperses and does not reform". The published
  variants — Elite, Phalanx, **Rabble**, Savage, Skirmishers — include the townsfolk case
  by name.
- **13th Age's mooks** supply the attrition the troop rules lack: a mob of mooks has "a
  collective HP pool equal to the sum of the HP of the mooks in the mob", and every time
  the mob takes one member's worth of damage, one member dies, with excess damage
  cascading. That is the player's "combined hp" exactly, and it makes the unit visibly
  shrink.
- **Morale** is where they run. Basic D&D's rule is the durable one: 2d6 against a morale
  score, checked [after the first death on a side and again when half are
  down](https://oldschoolessentials.necroticgnome.com/srd/index.php/Morale_(Optional_Rule)),
  and a side that passes twice fights to the death. Pathfinder has no general morale rule,
  which is why the troop can only be destroyed.

**Plan.** One new kind of actor, assembled from parts that already exist.

- **Shape.** A `troop` record on the actor: the member stat block, the member count, and
  a morale score. Hit points are members × member hp (13th Age). Size is set so the
  footprint holds the members — one 5-ft square each, so twelve raiders are a 4×4 huge
  unit with four squares to spare — which the grid and the map already draw.
- **Attrition.** Every member's worth of damage removes a member (13th Age), which lowers
  the count, shrinks the footprint and is the visible "scatter". At 0, the unit breaks up
  (Pathfinder).
- **Attacks out.** The troop's automatic damage, no attack roll (Pathfinder). This also
  solves a real engine problem: twelve raiders would otherwise be twelve NPC turns and
  twelve rolls a round.
- **Area and targeting.** Immune to "target one creature" effects, +50% from areas, saves
  once (Pathfinder). This is the rule that makes a fireball feel right against a crowd.
- **Morale.** A 2d6 check after the first member falls and again at half strength; a
  failure routs the unit — it leaves the board and the survivors scatter, which is the
  "run away" the player asked for.
- **XP.** Pay for the members who fell, not all-or-nothing: the dead are counted even if
  the rest ran. That needs `award_for_fallen` to read the troop's losses, and it is the
  honest answer to a rout.
- **Readout.** The token already draws; what it needs is the count and the pool — "17 of
  24" in the tooltip, a member count on the glyph, and a distinct fill so a unit of many
  does not read as one big creature.

This also settles item 5: a band of twelve raiders becomes one unit of twelve, not four
actors named "twelve soldier", and the promotion cap stops fighting the fiction.

## The plan, in order

Grouped so that each group can ship and be played before the next begins.

**1. The small true things** — items 6 and 7, plus item 1's price fix. The stranger gives
his name when asked and refuses for a reason; nobody stands in a scene undescribed; the
three free weapons get prices so the outfitter can stock them. All four are small, all
four are visible in the first ten minutes of play.

**2. Nobody is invented** — items 4 and 5. Ask the world before creating a person, and
give the three answers of scope. Widen the cast vocabulary, read the real number, let
prose promote during a fight, and hold a role word once it is booked. These belong
together: both are about who exists and on whose word.

**3. The crowd** — item 8, which completes item 5. The troop actor, its attrition, its
morale and its readout. Largely new code, but sitting on a grid, a renderer and a corpus
that are already there.

**4. The kit and the purse** — item 1 proper. Small, and safest after the outfitter has
been exercised by the price fix in group 1.

**5. The map and the roster** — item 3. Lay the ground on arrival, keep it, place by
zone; then one chooser for who a place holds, offered to the model instead of four
templates.

**6. Spellcasting, and the cleric's own class table** — items 2 and 2b, on their own, as
their own release. Make list-casters prepare,
build the screen, add the wizard's book and the cleric's conversion, put the prepared
spells in the brief, and add the check that catches a spell narrated but never cast.

**7. The other three hollow classes** — the rest of item 2b: the fighter's bonus feats and
training, the wizard's school or bond, the rogue's sneak attack and talents. Last because
sneak attack is a damage rule the engine applies rather than a document it reads, and
because none of it is in the way of play the way an uncastable cleric is.

A note on sequencing: group 6 is the biggest and the most user-visible, but it is last of
the ones that block play because groups 2, 3 and 5 all touch the same spawn and cast
paths, and doing them around a spellcasting rebuild would mean merging the two hardest
changes in the app at once.
