### 9. `/gm` hands over what the character should not know, with no gate

**Reported.** The first `/gm` answer of the session: "my notes indicate that there is a
shadow power in the form of an old veterans' league that everyone in office refuses to
cross." The player's ruling: the information should be *available*, but only after the
player is asked whether they want to be told what their character does not know yet.

**Found.** The export marks hiddenness in three places: every character carries a `Secret`
fact (all 256 in Aurvantis), the world has a foundational `secret` (the narrator brief
already guards it with "never state it outright"), and cards can be `secret`. Settlement
facts carry no flag; "Shadow Power" is hidden only by its key name, "Tension" and
"Volatility" read as rumour-grade, the rest are public. Leak paths in the shipped code:
`gm_search.dossier` writes every settlement fact and puts Shadow Power among the first
four for any "who runs" question; `gm_search.passages` writes every fact of a named
person, `Secret` included; the narrator's own brief already carries Shadow Power and
Tension and the `/gm` prompt inherits it.

**Research** (every quote checked against its source). The named error is the
Alexandrian's "Preempting Investigation". GUMSHOE: "If you ask for it, you will get it";
passive clues only "when you're bogging down". Blades in the Dark: "If it's common
knowledge, the GM will simply answer your questions", otherwise a roll, and "always
answers honestly, but with a level of detail according to the level of effect". PF1e
already encodes the split the player asked for: Knowledge (local) is what you *know* —
rulers and laws DC 10, common rumour DC 15, hidden organisations DC 20, untrained capped at
DC 10, "Try Again: No" — while Diplomacy (gather information) is *finding out*: "1d4
hours canvassing", DC 10 for common facts, "20 or higher" for secret knowledge, and it can
be retried. PF2e: after a failure "further attempts are fruitless", and "some groups find
it simpler to have players roll all secret checks and just try to avoid acting on any
out-of-character knowledge, while others enjoy the mystery" — the switch is the table's.
No solo or AI tool examined (Mythic, Ironsworn, AI Dungeon, NovelAI, SillyTavern, Friends
& Fables) has a spoiler-consent or player-knowledge gate at all. Disco Elysium's passives
fire silently at a threshold and red checks cannot be retried; BG3 added "Hide Passive
Rolls" as an opt-in set once per campaign after players objected to failed checks being a
tell. Abandoned elsewhere: rolling to find clues (GUMSHOE), percentage speech checks
(Sawyer, save-scumming), always-visible failed passives (BG3). The spoiler literature is
contradictory and none of it is about play; the one robust finding is that choosing a
spoiler is the individual's and does not hurt their enjoyment — which supports offering
the choice.

**Plan.** Tier facts by key in code — public / rumour / hidden, unknown keys default hidden;
`Secret` facts and secret cards are hidden. Public answered. Rumour: a secret Knowledge
(local) roll once per (character, fact), recorded so re-asking is fruitless; on a miss,
"nothing comes to mind" plus the in-play route (ask around: Diplomacy, 1d4 hours,
retryable). Hidden: never from `/gm` by default, a hook at most ("the town's answer is too
tidy") plus the in-play route. A campaign-level setting set once — "the GM may offer what
my character doesn't know" — turns hidden into the offer the player asked for: "Your
character wouldn't know this yet — say the word if you want it anyway, or find out in
play." Known trade-off, accepted: the offer itself tells the player a secret exists.
Hidden-tier facts also leave the model's passages and the refusal's notes.

### 23. RULING — what Continue means

**The player's words.** "Continue should work as I have nothing to add and continue the
scene, not as nobody does anything. My character should keep doing whatever he is doing
and the scene should move forward without any addition from me. If I am running a machine
and a fight breaks out, if I then hit continue my character should keep running the
machine as more of the fight plays out, and if I keep hitting continue the fight should
play out further and further, even coming to an end, having the authorities called. The
whole time I'm hitting continue I will be running the machine."

**Found.** The shipped design is the inverse (`gm/prompts.py` CARRY_ON and
CARRY_ON_EXAMPLES: "nobody acts, nothing new arrives").

**Plan.** The player's character *continues their standing action* — the thread's "doing"
— stated to the narrator as a fact of the beat, never dropped, never turned into a new
action. *The world advances one beat*: the engaged NPC goes on with what they were doing;
in a fight the round proceeds with the NPC turns rolled and the player's turn a legal delay
(or total defence, by setting); the watcher ticks; a card may escalate; time passes. Each
press advances further — a fight left to run reaches its end and its consequences on its
own. Continue is a *directive*, never an utterance: its text never reaches the planner as
the player's words, and `say`/`give` of the directive's own text are refused. The Continue
examples *show* a standing action held while the scene moves; none may show a stalled
room.

### 24. Class kits should become starting gold

**Reported.** "Remove the starting items from classes and give them extra starting gold,
we have the outfitter now."

**Found.** Eleven hand-written kits, `rules/creation.py:113-131`, read once at
`:576-609`; nothing else in the app grants gear, and a homebrew class already gets none.
Starting gold is **already rolled** per class (`creation.starting_purse`, `:201-232`), so
a character today gets the Core Rulebook's full starting wealth *and* a free kit on top —
which makes removing the kit the rulebook rather than a nerf: "each character begins play
with an outfit worth 10 gp or less" and nothing else. A fighter's kit prices at 172 gp
against an average roll of 175. Two shipped documents claim no gold is rolled and are
wrong (`rules/classbuilder.py:417`, `docs/class-creation.md:50`), and
`tests/test_classbuilder.py:114` pins the falsehood. **Blocker:** `quarterstaff` and
`club` carry `cost_gp: null` and `light crossbow` is absent from `weapons.json`, and the
outfitter skips anything costing nothing (`outfit_views.py:42`) — so five class kits are
built from weapons the shop cannot sell back.

**Plan.** Price the free weapons at 0 and let the outfitter stock a 0-gp item; import the
light crossbow. Then delete `KITS` and grant only the free outfit `OUTFITS` already gives.
The purse is untouched — it is already right, and the "extra gold" is the gold that was
always there and had been quietly pre-spent. Keep the objects that are class *features*
rather than equipment (the wizard's spellbook, a divine focus) or a caster cannot cast.
Link the outfitter from the roster so it can be re-entered on purpose.

### 25. Spellcasting: the declaration never reaches the engine

**Reported.** "Spellcasting is broken in general. A lvl 1 cleric can cast wall of flame at
6th level."

**Found — and the engine's gate is not the fault.** `_check_cast`
(`rules/engine.py:6555-6627`) makes nine checks including caster level, and refuses live:
*"Flame Strike is a level 5 spell and Ted is a level 1 cleric — they reach level 1."*
Nothing was cast. Proof from the player's own screen: after the wall of flame the panel
still read **Spell Slot 0: 3 of 3, Spell Slot 1: 4 of 4**. The chain: `prepare_from:
"list"` is read as "knows everything, prepares nothing" (`rules/casting.py:313-315`), so a
cleric's `spellbook` and `prepared` stay empty for the life of the character;
`inject_cast` builds its vocabulary from exactly those two fields
(`gm/judgement.py:3403-3417`) and bows out; no `cast` op is ever required, and out of
combat `narrate_only` is legal; and the brief tells the model nothing about spells at all
(`gm/prompts.py:860-945`). Wall of Fire is not even on the cleric list — its `lists` has
no cleric key.

**Plan.** Make `prepare_from: "list"` mean what it says: clerics, druids, paladins and
rangers prepare from the whole class list, and the prepared check applies to them. That
one change gives `inject_cast` a vocabulary and the working gate starts working. Then: the
rulebook's wizard book (all 0-level + 3 + Int modifier, two per level in
`rules/leveling.py`); spontaneous cure conversion (sacrifice a prepared spell of that
level or higher, never a domain slot); prepared spells and slots remaining in the brief;
and a new mechanical check — a beat naming a spell the engine did not cast is a finding,
rewritten once and cut as the backstop, the same shape as `wrong-hands`. Note the one
refinement on the reported rule: the book lets a lower-level spell fill a higher slot,
never the reverse, which satisfies both prohibitions and is what should ship.

### 26. The spells panel shows no known spells, and no way to prepare

**Reported.** "This spells panel should show a list of all known spells as well." The

Prepared today, *"Nothing in the book yet."*

**Found.** Every number on that page is right — three orisons and four first-level slots

`play/views.py:903-909` counts what is prepared at a level against the slots at that level.

**Plan.** A third region between Slots and Prepared today, fed by one new function
`casting.known_spells(actor)`: a wizard's book, a sorcerer's repertoire, or the whole

castable part is **179** (20 orisons, 159 first-level). Each castable row carries the

drifting into prose because nothing recognised it.

### 28. A map at all times, and spawns that fit the situation

`play/views.py:52` and in the browser, which prints "No ground is mapped." Nothing is
missing to derive one — `floorplan.for_place` is a pure function of place id, terrain and
authored shape, all available every turn, and its scatter is a SHA-seeded LCG, not
`random`. There is even a `floorplan.describe()` that nothing calls. What is absent is
**positions**: every placer returns early with no grid, though the zone word survives on
every actor. On spawns: there is **no randomness anywhere** in the spawn path. The
complaint is appropriateness — who appears is three regexes over the player's own sentence

**Plan.** Lay the floorplan on arrival rather than on violence, keep it through

does, with the tactical layer added by the fight. Then one chooser,
`who_would_be_here(scene, world)`, reading the place's `STAFFED` words, the live cards'
people, the settlement's residents and the place category in that order, resolved against
the bestiary by `npcs.choose` inside the CR band — and offered to the model in place of
the four templates.

### 29. "I turn to find the mayor" spawned a mayor

**Reported.** "A character who was not in the scene — the Narrator spawned them instead of
saying I couldn't find them." Seen again one beat after item 25's wall of flame: *"The
mayor stands before you, his heavy woolen cloak still smelling of woodsmoke."*

**Found.** Six repairs in `gm/judgement.py` can spawn somebody the player named, and
**not one of them consults the world**. The culprit is `repair_unknown_refs` (`:869`),
reproduced: the model writes a target `"mayor"`, validation raises `refs`,
`_INVENTED_REF` matches, and because exactly one ref was invented the ref becomes the
actor's *name* — a 13-hp Warrior-1 called *mayor*, carrying a sap. The engine can already
answer both halves of the question and is never asked: `World.by_name` / `World.residents`
(only caller is the `/gm` tool) and `_elsewhere`, which prints the right sentence but keys
on a ref, so a person never instantiated has none. The asymmetry is stark — a *sale* to an
absent person is dropped and the turn survives; an *attack* or a *talk* invents them. And
the world's answer here is good: no mayor exists among the 256 characters (the roles are
law-speaker 19, harbor-reeve 13, guildmaster 10), and every city's `Formal Power` names an
office and never a person.

**Plan.** Ask the world before anybody is created, and give parser fiction's three answers
of scope: **here**, nothing to do; **real but elsewhere**, "the harbour-reeve keeps the
quay; he is not here", by name rather than by ref, with the place named; **no such
person**, "there is no mayor in Vormoor; the town keeps to a reeve", built from the
settlement's own fact. Never a spawn. The narrow exception stays: `repair_unknown_refs`
exists because the *prose* described people arriving, and that is legitimate — the test is
whose word it was.

### 30. Raiders in the prose and nobody on the board

should have as many raiders spawned as is described … also they were initially described
as soldiers and then became raiders."

**Found, worse than reported.** `raider` is in no form in `_CAST_ROLES`
(`gm/judgement.py:3753-3767`). Measured: `"a band of twelve raiders"` books **zero** — no

the name, and a later beat calling them something else is repaired to it. The cap is the
real tension — four is right for extras and wrong for twelve raiders — and item 33
resolves it.

### 31. The stranger refuses to give his name

**Reported.** "Stranger on the stairs refuses to give his name." Seen again on the wall of

**Plan.** On the turn the player asks — `judgement._ASKS_A_NAME` already detects it — the

better gives a name, unfriendly or hostile refuses, and a Diplomacy check can move them.
Refusing stays legitimate — the player said so — but becomes legible and actionable.

### 32. People stand in the scene undescribed

**Reported.** "Drenn Ironvale and the merchant are in scene without having been

**Found.** Four writers put people into a scene — the opening companion

### 33. A crowd as one thing

**Reported.** "A crowd of people should be spawned as a single unit with the combined hp
of all its members, it should take up however many squares the people would take up, find
a way to display this and allow for them to be killed giving xp, run away and scatter."

**Found.** The only collective code does the opposite on purpose:
`bestiary.split_collective_name` turns "a pair of guards" into two actors, and its
docstring records why — a single 11-hp actor called "pair of guards" meant the player
"fought half as many people as the fiction described". Right for two guards, wrong for a
crowd. Three things make the reversal cheap: the grid **already** honours multi-square
creatures (`grid.footprint`, `tables.SPACE_AND_REACH`: 1 square medium, 4 large, 9 huge,
16 gargantuan, and every reader of distance, reach, cover and flanking respects it); the
map **already** draws them (`table.html:2330-2351` renders an `n × n` rect from
`a.squares`, served at `play/views.py:206`); and the corpus **already** ships 22 `troop`
and 41 `swarm` stat blocks, whose `subtype` is stripped before the Actor is built so the
engine has never known a troop is a troop. What is missing is the rule set. `xp.worth`
pays one all-or-nothing award per fallen actor and nothing at all for a rout.

**Plan.** One new kind of actor from three traditions. **Pathfinder's troop subtype** for
the shape: the unit occupies contiguous squares, makes no attack rolls but deals automatic
damage to anyone in reach, saves as a single creature, is immune to effects targeting a
number of creatures, takes +50% from areas, and "reducing a troop to 0 hit points or fewer
causes it to break up". **13th Age's mooks** for the attrition the troop rules lack — a
shared pool equal to the sum of the members, and every member's worth of damage kills one,
which shrinks the footprint and is the visible scatter. **Basic D&D's morale** for running
away: 2d6 against a morale score, checked after the first death and again at half
strength, two passes meaning fight to the death. XP pays for the members who fell even if
the rest ran, which is the honest answer to a rout and needs `award_for_fallen` to read
the unit's losses. The token already draws; it needs the member count, the pool, and a
fill that does not read as one big creature. This also settles item 30: a band of twelve
raiders is one unit of twelve, not four actors named "twelve soldier".

### 35. Four travel ops in one plan walk the party across town

allows a second person when the description differs ("the man in the leather apron" after

on the board on 2026-09-18). Here the beat named the same merchant twice, once with a
participle.

**Plan.** Not obviously fixable by the same rule that created it, which is why it is its
own item: the two cases are "same role word, different description" and the dedup cannot
tell them apart from the words alone. The likely answer is the sentence — two mentions in
ONE sentence describing one action are one person — and it belongs with item 33's unit
work, where the ledger's counting is being rebuilt anyway.

## The plan for the 2026-09-19 items, in order

Grouped so each can ship and be played before the next begins. Same discipline as above:
one branch off master, the full suite, a live check through `/api/say` on a copy of the
