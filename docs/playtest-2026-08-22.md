# Playtest: two sessions, two narrators

2026-08-22, played through the real HTTP loop (`/api/say`, `/api/roll`, the crafting
bench's own endpoints) as a player would, one full session per candidate narrator.
Campaign saves: `kesst-vayr-3.json` (qwen) and `kesst-vayr-4.json` (llama) in the user
campaigns directory — the transcripts are readable end to end.

Both sessions: Kesst Vayr, rogue 1, the shipped Pangrella opening.

## Verdict on the narrators

**llama3.1:8b ships.** Not because it is good — because it keeps the game in front of
the rules, and the other candidate does not.

| | qwen3-4b-abliterated | llama3.1:8b |
|---|---|---|
| latency/turn | 5–7s | 10–40s |
| answers the question asked | rarely | yes |
| proposes checks | wrong ones, railroaded | sensible, but too few |
| "I attack" reaches the engine | **never** | yes |
| narrates for the player | whole days at a time | occasionally (invented a bow) |
| repeats itself | verbatim paragraphs, 3× | no |
| prompt echo into transcript | catastrophic (guarded now) | no |

The 4B failed in ways no prompt will fix: it recycled its own tin-shop paragraph word
for word across three turns, defused every fight I picked, resolved an explicit "I draw
my dagger and attack" entirely in prose with no intent, and ended the session emitting
percentage-chance game-manual text with an emoji. Speed is worth nothing at that price.

## What the sessions proved about the app (model-independent)

Ordered by how much each one breaks the game.

1. **The GM narrates a cast the engine does not have.** The conspirators, the cowering
   woman, the winged stranger — none were ever actors. Only two things existed all
   session: the PC and the town guildhand. So when I attacked the winged woman in the
   forest, target-filling routed the blow to the only real NPC — the dying gatekeeper
   dragged along from the city — and llama then *retconned the fiction to agree*
   ("it's Zara, the guildhand who was supposed to be watching the gate"). Either the GM
   gets a spawn op it is pushed to use, or an attack on somebody who is not in
   `scene.actors` must be refused the way other invalid intents are.

2. **The player was never once in danger.** Two sessions, a stabbing, a flight from the
   city, a forest ambush — and no hostile actor ever attacked, because no hostile actor
   ever existed. `fighting` stayed `None` through both sessions even as a man was run
   through to -4 hp.

3. **The survival system is unreachable from the table.** "I sleep until morning" charged
   20 minutes (qwen) and then 0 minutes (llama); "I eat from my rations and drink" reset
   nothing. Only the foraging endpoint charges real time (8 hours foraged = 480 real
   minutes, verified). The sleep/food/water rules built this week can only ever fire on
   a player who forages constantly and never sleeps — the opposite of their intent. The
   GM needs a pass-time/rest op, or `sleep`/`eat` phrasing needs mechanical detection.

4. **Scenes never shed actors.** The gatekeeper wounded in the guild yard travelled to
   the forest inside the scene and took a "holds back" NPC turn after every single
   player turn for the rest of the session.

5. **Setup narration pre-resolves attacks.** "Your blade bites deep into her side" and
   "your dagger still lodged in the arm" both printed *before* the attack roll was
   offered. `find_outcome_claims` missed both shapes.

6. **Both models parrot a stale `because`.** "going over the wall while the lamp is
   away" attached to a social question one turn later (qwen); "you've got an opponent
   down" attached to three different attacks across two scenes (llama). The recent-turn
   history invites copying; the `because` of a previous intent should probably not be
   shown to the next call verbatim.

7. **A failed check can be narrated as success.** Intimidate missed by 8; the
   consequence read "your grip holds her silent" — a success in fiction. The tell was
   right and the prose overrode it.

8. **Narrated travel does not move the engine.** Fled the city to the treeline; biome
   stayed `urban` until `/api/travel` was called by hand. Fiction and forage tables
   disagreed until then.

9. **State the UI cannot show:** `clock` and `location` come back empty in `/api/state`
   even when `scene.clock_minutes` is 480 and the biome is set. The player cannot see
   time pass or where they are.

10. **Inert content:** both crafted ingredients carried `effects: []`, so the salve is
    mechanically nothing. Money exists only in prose — llama paid me five silver that
    no ledger recorded.

## Fixed during the playtest

- The consequence call now passes through `clean_consequence` — scaffold echo, worked-
  example plagiarism, verbatim loops, letter-free residue all cut mechanically, with
  the engine's own tells as the fallback. The worked example moved off the fixture's
  scene (a ferry, nowhere in the shipped world) so a copied answer can be caught.
- Narrator pinned to llama3.1:8b in settings, with the session evidence in the comment.
- Watcher row set to deepseek-r1:8b — still wired to nothing, still labelled.

## What to build next, in order

1. A `spawn`/`appear` op the GM emits when its fiction introduces somebody — plus the
   refusal: an attack on a name not in the scene bounces with "there is nobody by that
   name here", which also pushes the model to spawn.
2. A `rest`/`pass_time` op so sleeping, eating and drinking reach the survival clocks.
3. Scene transitions that drop dead and distant actors (and set `fighting`).
4. Show `clock` and `location` in `/api/state`.
5. Teach `find_outcome_claims` the "blade bites deep / still lodged" shapes.
6. Stop feeding prior intents' `because` strings back into the next call's context.
