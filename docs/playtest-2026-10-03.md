# Playtest, 2026-10-03: Kesst in Zhilvarnia (0.2.6)

The owner played **Kesst Vayr**, a level-1 rogue, in Pangrella (Zhilvarnia), on 0.2.6.
Every model call was `igorls/gemma-4-12B-it-heretic-GGUF`. Two zips of autosaves came back:

- `json.zip`, called **market-talk** here: 8 player turns in the market.
- `itemsfromnarration.zip`, called **items**: the same save 15 turns later, covering the docks,
  a warehouse, the counting house and a smithy.

The saves are the owner's and are never committed. For investigation they sit in the session
scratchpad, `jsonzip/slice.json*` and `items/slice.json*`. Each newer file holds all of the
older ones, and `.3` is the oldest.

The owner said: *"there are a plethora of errors"*, then *"fix all of these issues"*.

Every item below was read off the save's `turn_log`, `ledger` and actor state, not just
the prose.

## Lane A: things have an owner, a source and a destination

The final inventory reads `goods: {"brunt of the weight": 1, "crate": 1, "pouch": 1,
"coins": 1}` and `purse: {}`.

1. **A take creates the thing from nothing.** `inject_goods` turned each "I take X" into
   `give X to pc` with no `from_`.
   - "I take **the brunt of the weight** and move it…" made an item called *brunt of the
     weight*.
   - The crate the docks man (`c9`) was carrying was copied rather than moved.
2. **A sold crate is still carried.** Four haggling turns resolved to `narrate_only`. No
   `sell` was planned, because `_op_sell` and `inject_sale` only know crafted `stock`, not
   `goods`. The crate never left the player, and no price was ever named.
3. **The money is an item called "coins".** "I pocket the coins" became `give coins`, which
   is goods, not purse. The next turn planned `give gp 1 from pc` and got "Kesst Vayr has no
   gp to give."
4. **A pouch is not a container.** "Transfer the coins from the pouch into my coin purse" did
   nothing, and the pouch stays in goods next to the coins it held.
5. **A drop copies the thing to a bystander.** "I drop the Brunt of the weight on the ground"
   was planned as `give … to c13` (the smith) with no `from_`.
   - The smith gained a new *Brunt of the weight*.
   - The player kept *brunt of the weight*, because the names differ only in case.
   - There is no ground to drop things on.
6. **The sale ignored the rules.** "75% of the crate's value" passed with no appraisal and no
   price. In Pathfinder 1e, goods sell for half their price.
7. **The narration made Kesst take a stranger's pack.** *"You step into the heavy pack and haul
   the weight onto your own shoulders… the merchant's eyes widen"*. Nothing recorded the pack
   as his, and nobody reacted.
8. **Market-talk: a wink was planned as a `give`.** "Kesst Vayr has no friendly wink to give",
   and the ledger says "handed something to Kesst Vayr". The give was overruled and still
   reached the tell and the ledger.

## Lane B: where and when

9. **A verb of intent founds and enters a place.**
   - "I take the brunt of the weight and move it toward **the storage area**" founded *the
     storage area* off the docks and moved the player into it.
   - "head for **the side door**" founded *the smithy* and moved the player there.
   - Both times the first plan was refused because the place does not exist. The retry
     founded it instead of asking.
10. **Leaving people "mid-sentence".** Every travel tell says "You leave X mid-sentence",
    even when nobody was speaking.
11. **The time of day runs backwards.**
    - The opening says **evening** at `clock_minutes 0`, and the clock ends at 88 minutes.
    - The prose goes: morning work at the docks → "evening shift, three hours from now" →
      "before the morning shift arrives" → "the first hint of dawn" → "pre-dawn gloom".
    - Market-talk's last turn said "midnight".
12. **The setting drifts.** The game holds the player at `the-market`. The market-talk prose
    keeps placing them in a tavern ("the tavern patrons", "leans back against the bar").
    The opening frame says "a lit doorway with a room's noise behind it".

## Lane C: people and names

13. **The player's quoted words became a person.**
    - "I give a friendly wink and say 'just trying to start a conversation…'" was read as
      `talk target='say "just trying…"'`, and `introduce` minted actor `c4` with that name.
    - It prints in prose ("The say \"just trying…\" is a Korvu"), in tells and in the ledger.
    - It is still being spoken to 10 turns later.
    - Existing saves carry this actor, so loading must repair them.
14. **A people's name became a personal name.** The narrator called the warehouse laborer
    "a man named **Korvu**" (Korvu is a people of this world), and `c11` is now named
    *Korvu*. The source is the injected face line "The merchant with a heavy pack **is a
    Korvu**: …".
15. **Lines and faces land on the wrong person.**
    - In market-talk, every line from "the man" was tagged as the jug-carrying servant
      (`c1`).
    - That fired `greets-known-as-stranger` against the servant.
    - One paragraph pins `c6`'s face (wet-rope hair, ink-dot knuckles) on "the man in the
      heavy coat", who has `c4`'s missing tooth.
16. **The narrator speaks for the PC at an ordinary table.** The planner's invented `say` was
    caught and dropped ("a say in words the player never wrote"). The prose then gave Kesst a
    long invented line anyway: *"A piece of work like this," you say, your voice dropping into
    a sultry lilt…*

## Lane D: the turn pipeline

17. **Talk turns loop on "the same plan as last turn".**
    - Two market-talk turns spent 5 attempts each, 85 s and 76 s, rejected by
      `judgement.py:324`'s repeat guard.
    - A conversation turn's plan is legitimately `narrate_only + say` every time.
18. **The fallback model is not installed.**
    - "handing the turn to richardyoung/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507-abliterated", then "could not
      be reached: … HTTP Error 404".
    - A 404 is "model not pulled", not "Ollama down". The wording is wrong, and the handoff
      should be skipped when the model is not on the host.
19. **A polish rewrite ships what the prose checks would have cut.** Market-talk's last turn:
    - the first draft repeated the previous turn, and after "repeated beats: cut 12" only
      24 characters were left;
    - the second polish shipped with `(c1)` and `(c4)` on the page, said "midnight", and was
      cut off mid-sentence ("…sell you a'. What do you do?").
20. **"The clerk's eyes drop to the floor" read as the clerk down or dead**
    (`contradicts-the-engine`). The repair also cut the draft below the minimum length.
21. **"Declared and not shown" when the drop was shown.** "You release your grip on the heavy
    weight and let it fall" did not count as showing "drop the Brunt of the weight on the
    ground", and the draft was kept as written.
22. **The rolling memory loses what happened.**
    - The context budget drops 28 of 64 messages by the end, and the ledger is all that
      carries them.
    - Its entries read "handed something to Kesst Vayr", "you spoke with someone" and "you
      went to ca~urban:the-docks" (a raw id).
    - The crate, the sale, the price and the clerk's deal are not there.

## Lane E: rules and the opening

23. **Diplomacy DC.** The flirt-and-sell was a Diplomacy check at a generic "average" DC 10,
    minus 2 for circumstance, which made it DC 8. A natural 4 (total 10) succeeded.
    - Pathfinder 1e sets the DC by attitude: 15 + Cha modifier for an indifferent creature.
    - Haggling a price is not a Diplomacy request.
24. **`/gm what is the veil?` answered with the spell *Veil*.** The narrator itself had
    suggested "I explain why I am looking for the veil". A question about something the
    story mentioned should be answered from the story first, or said to be unknown, before
    the rulebook is consulted.
25. **The opening card never moves.**
    - "Find a bed you can pay for" collected 5 approaches and never advanced.
    - The watcher refused facts as "not about this card".
    - No inn or room was ever put in reach, and the player drifted into a dock job with the
      bed forgotten.
26. **Each turn costs 18–58 s planning plus 20–35 s per prose or polish call.** This is not a
    defect on its own; every lane should avoid adding model calls.

### What the live check found after the lanes were green

The merged branch was played in the running app on copies of both saves, in throwaway data
directories, against the same gemma-4-12B. Every lane's tests were green, and the first
eleven live turns still turned up eleven defects. All of them are now fixed, each with a
test built from its live line:

1. **A walk across the room was cut.** "walk over toward the anvil" was read as a refused
   journey, and the beat ended "You are still at the smithy". `refused_move` now asks the
   planner's own `movement_within`.
2. **A second deed in one sentence was never read.** "I pick the crate back up, then tip
   the coins…": every deed reader anchors on "I <verb>". `as_declarations` splits the
   sentence on then / and then / ; and on "and" before an item verb, and turns "pick X
   up" into "pick up X".
3. **Coin blocked the crate.** The coins' give was counted as the turn's one acquisition,
   so the crate was never picked up. Coin and goods now each cover only their own kind.
4. **The saves still carried the minted goods.** On load, *brunt of the weight* is now
   removed, and *coins* go into the purse at the engine's one-copper rate.
5. **A thing still held was handed away in the prose.** "the smith… takes the crate from
   you". New check: `gm/checks/thing_kept.py`.
6. **A people's name was used as a person's name on the page.** "the smith, Korvu, reaches
   out". New check: `gm/checks/peoples_name.py`.
7. **A sale with no actor raised `KeyError: None`.** The player saw "The engine refused
   the GM's intents: None" and lost the turn. `sell`, `buy` and `use_item` now default to
   the player; `cast` is refused.
8. **A refused sale was settled in the prose.** "The transaction is finalized. The heavy
   clink of the coin" was written while the smith's counter was shut. New check:
   `gm/checks/trade_claimed.py`; its backstop puts the engine's refusal on the page.
9. **The second fault in one sentence shipped.** When two checks flag one sentence, the
   lighter finding is dropped as covered. Its member was never asked again, so "Korvu
   eyes the crate" shipped. The covered members now get the backstop pass.
10. **Asking again for what was refused counted as a repeat.** The guard now accepts a
    plan whose every acting op is backed by the reading of this turn's own words.
11. **A deal spoken aloud never closed.** The close was read with speech redacted, there
    was no buyer when nobody was named, and "He is a large man" had made a second smith.
    Now:
    - the close and the buyer are read with speech in;
    - the person in conversation counts as the buyer;
    - a predicate after he/she is/was is nobody new.

Proven live at the end: at 01:28 the sale of the crate was refused and the refusal reached
the page. After waiting until 06:00, "I tell the smith, \"It's a deal. You can have the
crate.\"" sold it for 7 sp 5 cp, half of its 1.5 gp. The crate went to the smith, the
purse read 7 sp 7 cp, and the ledger said "sold the crate to the smith".

  enters… puts his club down", and the smith was already there. That made c14 "man".

- **The fallback model's 404 in the owner's save is unexplained.** That model

- **Nobody reacts to a theft yet** (lane A). Things the prose puts in someone's hands are
