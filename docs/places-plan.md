# Places — one spatial coordinate, everything else derived

The plan of record for the place model. Written after a six-tradition survey of how
interactive fiction, MUDs, tabletop systems, LLM game masters, text-game research and
game engines solve movement within and between spaces, plus an adversarial critic and a
mapping pass over this codebase.

## The diagnosis

The engine holds **four independent spatial authorities**, none derived from any other:

| authority | what it is | who writes it |
|---|---|---|
| `Scene.location_id` | a World Bible entity id (a city) | campaign setup |
| `Scene.spot` | **free text** naming a room | a model-supplied `travel` param |
| `Scene.biome` | a closed 14-value enum | `travel`, and an injector |
| `Scene.zones` / `positions` / `grid` | tactical distance | combat |

They can and do disagree, and the reported failure is exactly that: a merchant's stall and
the square outside are the *same* `location_id` and the *same* `biome`, so walking out
changed nothing the engine could see. This is the shape of every earlier stage of the
compliance programme — one clock, one vocabulary, one applicator — in space.

**And `Scene.spot`, added 2026-09-01, is the worst of the four.** It is an undeclared
string space: nothing can compare it, index a rule on it, or refuse it. It is written
verbatim from a model param, which is the narrator *establishing* a fact rather than
proposing one — the exact inversion this project exists to prevent. It also gave "where
the party is" a **second writer**: `scene.spot` (a model param) and `scene.thread["where"]`
(a regex over the player's own sentence), and `scene_brief` prints both into one prompt, so
a single brief can assert two different rooms. `docs/intent-protocol.md` §8: *no field has
two writers*.

## What the traditions agree on

Every one of them holds **exactly one spatial relation, and derives presence from it**:
Inform's containment (`in` / `on` / `part of`, with `location of` walking up the tree),
Diku's `IN_ROOM`, LambdaMOO's `location`/`contents`, the Z-machine's single `parent`,
Evennia's `location`, Godot's rooms owning their occupants. Nobody stores a roster beside
a place. This app stores `Scene.actors` as a roster, which is why walking out needed the
merchant shed *by hand* — and why `depart`, `leave_behind`, `tidy_the_fallen` and travel
each carry their own copy of that logic.

Other load-bearing agreements:

- **Terrain is read off the place, never a peer of it.** Diku reads `SECT(room)`; Inform
  derives a region from the destination room. `biome` as a sibling field is what let
  "both are urban" defeat the transition.
- **The sub-place is a closed enumeration, not free text.** Inform's Recipe Book 3.3:
  positions within a room are "a kind of value", "a small number of named positions".
  Fate caps a conflict at "two to four zones".
- **Exits are enumerated and the engine refuses anything off the list** — the move
  vocabulary *is* the exit list.
- **Adjacency yes, edge weights no.** Fate shipped weighted zone borders and deleted them.
- **Montfort's boundary test**: two places are different rooms iff a move is required
  before certain objects can be manipulated. You cannot buy from the merchant while
  standing in the square, so they are different rooms. The engine was right to need
  something; it had nowhere to put it.

## What is deliberately refused

- **Compass directions as the exit vocabulary.** Every tradition that uses them assumes a
  parser and a player with a mental map. The live sentence was "I leave the building and
  search the city" — no direction, and there never will be one. What transfers is *the set
  of exits is closed*; what does not is *the set is the compass*.
- **Weighted edges or traversal cost.** Fate's deleted experiment.
- **A model minting place names at runtime.** That is what free-text `spot` already is.
  The app generates places; the model only ever chooses one that exists.
- **A coordinate grid for outdoor space.** There is already a tactical coordinate system;
  a second would be a fifth spatial authority.
- **Place as an `ActiveEffect`.** A naive reading of law 2 pushes this way and it is
  wrong: a place has no duration, no stacking, and removing it must not leave the party
  nowhere. Place is a **KIND** — a scene-held dataclass with `as_dict`/`from_dict`,
  the `Manifestation` pattern.
- **Separate `travel_to` / `travel_within` ops.** The distinction is real but must be
  *computed by the engine from the destination*, never declared — two ops would let the
  narrator assert "this was only a small move", which is the bug.

## Both authors, one format

World Bible cannot author every place, so a place must be creatable here. That is not a
licence for the model to invent one: places are **generated by the app, deterministically,
seeded off the location's durable entity id**, so the same town has the same rooms across
every session and reload — and later *authored* by World Bible, which is planned to ship
towns and the places in them. One format, two authors, and in neither case does the model
establish anything: it chooses from what exists, and is refused if it does not.

## Stages

**8a — a place is a thing with an identity.** `rules/places.py`: a `Place` KIND (id, name,
terrain, exits) and `spots_for(location, world)` generating a closed, seeded set per
location. Held on the Scene, saved, stated in the brief the way refs already are ("these
are the only ones that exist"). `travel` refuses an unknown destination as a printable
Outcome, in the `_ability_refusal` shape. No containment yet.

**8b — one coordinate.** Actors are *in* a place. `Scene.actors` becomes a derived view of
who is here, so "who is present" is a query and the shedding stops being hand-written.
Measured: 363 reads of `.actors` against **11 writes, 5 of them in production** — the
roster is already funnelled through `add`/`depart`, which is what makes this affordable.

**8c — terrain is derived.** `biome` is read off the current place rather than stored
beside it, and stops being a thing `travel` sets.

**8d — one writer for where.** `thread["where"]` defers to the place; the brief states it
once.

**8e — the ratchet.** A law test that there is one spatial authority and that nothing
writes a place name that did not come from the registry.

Campaigns may be flushed rather than migrated — the user's explicit call, so the model is
built right rather than around the twelve existing saves.
